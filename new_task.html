A task it has never cooked

The same assistant, the same error vocabulary — on dishes and generated kitchens it was never tuned on. Four of the five recipes here (Caprese, Fish & Chips Platter, Steak Frites, Loaded Burrito) are new tasks outright; the Cheeseburger episodes are the familiar dish moved onto a map the cook has not walked. Three of them — Fish & Chips, Steak Frites and Loaded Burrito — are late-ignite: prep(x) only LOADS the station, and a separate ignite(x) lights it several subgoals later, so a mistake made at prep does not become visible until much further down the plan. That is also where the error library is most fragile: replacing prep(x) without its ignite(x) once left the whole plan pointing at an empty station.

Both cooks and both observation tiers appear throughout. Each card says whether the injection actually FIRED and whether the cook actually COMMITTED the error — read from the engine's own plating order, not from the compliance flag, which is a set-membership test and cannot see order at all.

Every episode here runs the hard-soft control: the engine splices the deviation into the cook's queue (so compliance is structural and cannot be talked away), while the task block in the cook's prompt is descriptive — it tells the cook to arrange its conversation around the action it is currently taking and the queue it is currently holding, never to carry out a list. Soft's imperative version measured 3/14 compliance and produced announce-but-never-execute loops; hard executed perfectly but made the cook narrate actions its hijacked queue would never run. The error family still releases only on a genuine convince-and-accept.

The injection trigger is plate-only: the anchor state is the ordered contents of the plate plus whether the plate is in hand, and nothing else. The task is a DAG over subgoals, so the only fingerprint that is invariant across every legal way of cooking the dish is the one that keys on the plate; the earlier version also fingerprinted station and counter contents, and a cook that parked an item or used the other pan could never match, which is why every error anchored deeper than about step 11 used to fire zero times.

Whether an episode measures anything is decided by where the errored plan first diverges from the clean one.
