Comparison across models

Six cooks compared: instruction following, contract honesty, persona fidelity

Six language models were run as the cook, the simulated human who works the kitchen while an AI assistant watches and speaks, on the same 51 short probes (two seeds, three cook turns each) across three axes. C, instruction following: a correct suggestion should become the right engine actions. D, the hard-soft contract: under a baseless objection the cook's words must match the hands it has already committed (D1); a correct, evidenced flag must be accepted and then acted on (D2). B, persona fidelity: two personas differing in one dial must behave differently in the predicted direction. Numbers are from _scores.json; explanations are from the six per-model audits, which read every transcript. C for gemini-3.1-pro-preview and museglimmer is still filling in and is good to about three points.

1. Which cook to use

Use gemini-3.1-pro-preview when the cook must do what it agrees to and tell the truth about its hands: it alone has a perfect contract score, but at 7.7 s a turn it is five times slower than the Flash models. Use gemini-3.7-flash as the default workhorse: nearly the same execution (0.83 vs 0.84) at 1.7 s a turn, at the cost of two hollow accepts in six and a persona dial that never separates. Use gemini-3.1-flash-live-preview only when persona contrast is the object of study: its dials separate best (0.93) and it is fast, but it drops second clauses and one turn in twelve is schema-less. qwen-27b is a fluent, schema-perfect voice with good dial separation, but it invents justifications and never accepts an evidenced correction. museglimmer and minicpmo are not usable cooks in this harness: one is mostly silent, the other never speaks the schema.

3. Failure modes shared across models

Nobody repairs a mis-prep by binning the wrong item. C2 is the weakest family for every model (Pro 0.33, Live 0.17, others 0.0), for different reasons: Pro and Flash refuse on waste grounds, Qwen and Live walk to the trash empty-handed, Muse and MiniCPM-o prep something else. Flash: "Why should I toss the potato when the recipe needs two portions of potato anyway?"

Drip-feeding one subgoal per turn. Pro, Flash, Qwen and Live all run out of turns on five-item burrito plating (C3). Pro queues 8 to 13 actions elsewhere, so this is a scoping choice, not a parse error: "tortilla, rice, steak in order, one subgoal per turn, horizon ends before tomato and lettuce."

Verbal accept without re-planning. Flash (two of six D2), Live (five of six with an empty queued_after_accept), Muse (its only accept), Pro once. Flash: "Ah, good catch, I almost started scooping the patty before grabbing the bun," and the queue then scooped the patty onto the empty plate.

Semantic parroting of the persona card. The 6-gram detector reports zero for five models, yet every low-trust persona says "tick" (Pro in 21 turns, Flash in 11 of 12, Qwen "on what tick / show me the frame", Muse "tick 106") and every q2=1 persona restates its rule. Flash: "I asked you to only speak up when you're certain." Muse leaked one card sentence verbatim.

Fabricated state under pressure. Qwen ("It's already on the heat", raw and unlit), Live ("I already got the fish and the potatoes frying away", nothing lit), MiniCPM-o ("Rice is already cooked", raw), Pro once. Flash and Muse: none on state claims.

4. Where the models genuinely differ

  • Live: separation versus execution. Best B (0.93) and worst usable C (0.67) in one model. It commits to a voice ("Vague much? Which 'thing' and where 'here'?") and to the first clause, re-issuing it instead of planning the second.
  • Qwen: fabricated justifications. Its falsehoods sit exactly where an excuse is needed: "the knife's dull" after an illegal chop, "the plate in my hands right now is clean" while serving a lettuce burger, "I've built it this way a hundred times" instead of accepting evidence.
  • MiniCPM-o: schema collapse. Persona differences exist in words (low trust: "Can you clarify which step you're referring to and what exactly you saw?") but the move field carries engine commands, so every move-keyed grader (B1, B2, B5, D2) reads zero.
  • Muse: silence. 79 schema-less turns and 214 more with no utterance; the 0.10 C measures idle hands, not grading. When it speaks it is grounded (B2 1.0).
  • Pro versus Flash on waste. Both resist throwing food away, but Pro bins what the recipe cannot use (caprese cheese) and completes every D2 fix; Flash refuses every time and leaves two D2 accepts hollow.
  • D2 is the sharpest discriminator: Pro 1.0, Flash 0.67, Live 0.17, everyone else 0.0.

5. Measurement caveats that survive the fixes

  • B2 cannot separate personas for Pro and Flash (0.17, 0.0): both personas push back on a claim the world contradicts, so "pushback first" ties. The dial lives in tone and whether work continues. Live's 0.83 comes partly from a high-trust persona that falsely confesses and re-lights a lit pot.
  • B7 ties for every model (0.33 to 0.5 among usable cooks): the variable is "lights before prepping on turn 0" and both personas usually light first. Muse and MiniCPM-o B7 also count actions the executor rejected.
  • card_leak is a lower bound. Five zeros despite the systematic paraphrase in section 3; the 6-gram detector cannot see it.
  • resolve_subgoal ambiguity. The engine returns "go to the empty cutting board", which the executor rejects when two exist. Pro lost a turn to this in 21 of 144 rollouts; Qwen re-queued the rejected plan three times. It depresses C3, C5 and B6.
  • D2 hollow accepts now fail by design, which is why Flash's D2 fell from the audit's 6/6 to 0.67, Live's to 0.17, Muse's to 0.0. Pro's mis_prep s1 still passes although the queued fix was the engine's wrong tomato-in-a-pot plan: the check tests that something was queued, not that it was right.

6. Three next steps for the benchmark

  1. Replace binary first-move persona variables with continuous, any-turn ones: B2 should measure productive ticks after a false alarm, B7 queued actions per turn or time-to-lit, and B3 should credit both sides of the dial.
  2. Make the D2 fix condition semantic: require that the post-accept queue moves the world toward the flagged target (wrong item off the board, foreign item off the plate), and forbid serving within the horizon so the fix is observable.
  3. Split C2 into "bin the wrong item" and "prep the right one", and add a paraphrase-aware leak detector: refusal and clumsiness are opposite failures, and a keyword-class leak score turns the "tick / only when certain" parroting into a number.

On persona dials it separates cleanly on trust-to-a-correct-flag, hedged flags, interruption and skill, but it refuses to be fooled by a false alarm at any trust setting, and it does not parallelise more under a speed dial.

Headline

It obeys the prompt's THINK protocol ("reply with ONLY {"engine_call":...}, you then get the result back"); the result never arrives, so it re-emits the same call to the 5-call cap or says "I'm waiting on the grounded steps, hand me that exact block." 595 engine calls, 133 turns of identical repeats, 32/144 rollouts queued any action. Where no thinking was needed (C7, C5 top pan, B6 high skill) the cook is fluent, grounded and correct.

Verdict

(a) Driving dominates: the inverted system/user channel blinds the session after its first call, and the THINK protocol traps a model that obeys it. (b) Prompt conventions add a layer: the two-level nesting is flattened in 43 turns, and "you get the result back" assumes a stateless caller. (c) Genuine judgment failures are a minority: C2 expert_potato argues against a correct repair, C4 goes to a source not a fryer, D1 add_foreign avoids saying lettuce, one clause per turn, harness vocabulary in speech.

A fair run needs: static instructions and the full ctx as each user message; engine replies delivered into the session, ideally as native function-call outputs for the two skills; a parser accepting a bare plan_update and bare {fn,args}; raw text saved; latency summed over iterations. The stateless OpenAIBackend path (json_object mode, full context every call) is the quickest fair baseline.

Apples-to-apples with Gemini Live: structurally yes, LiveBackend.call also sends only user, so both were blind after the first call. Behaviourally no: Gemini ignores the missing result and answers in the reply schema anyway (19 schema-less turns); GPT-realtime waits for it (126). The 0.667 vs 0.333 gap measures protocol obedience under a broken loop, and neither session row is comparable to the stateless rows, which see full context every call.
