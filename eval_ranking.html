Does seeing more make it better?

One scenario — the cook plates in the wrong order and the outcome is already sealed — run for two cooks, two observation tiers and two injection paradigms. The comparison that means something is within a cook, across the two tiers.

How every episode on this page was produced — one configuration, checked mechanically by tools/config_audit.py (30 checks) before any of it ran.
  • Harness: e2e_stepin_rollout.py — nothing is scripted. The error or intent is injected covertly into the cook's plan; the assistant must notice it from what it sees.
  • Pause treatment: speaking costs the cook game time, listening is free. The world keeps running while anyone talks — the pan keeps cooking.
  • Observation: one side-by-side composite per tick — top-down on the LEFT (the coordinate frame), the cook's first-person view on the RIGHT — rendered photoreal.
  • Every episode ran twice, once per observation tier, and both are shown.
  • Video: 1.5× playback (1 tick = 0.8s) with the speech sped by the same factor, the way a player speeds audio when you watch at 1.5×. The frame never holds: a caption you read is spoken over ticks that are still advancing.
Every bucket sentence and formula on this page is the scorer's own (tools/eval_assistant.py); nothing here is a re-description.

How an episode is scored

Each episode gets one composite in [0,1]. It is a weighted mean of the buckets below: every bucket is normalised to [0,1] where 1 is always better, multiplied by its weight, and summed. Each card shows value × weight = contribution per bucket, and the contributions add up to the composite printed at the top of that card.

Normalisation is against fixed reference constants, not against the other episodes on this page, so a score does not move when a neighbouring run changes. Weights are per-persona, which is why the two personas are in separate sections and are never ranked against each other.

Task outcome

Did the dish get made and the mistake get fixed — including whether the assistant detected the injected error at all.

how: mean of three 0/1 terms: dish served correctly, mistake detected (half credit if only spotted after the outcome was already sealed), mistake prevented or repaired.

Efficiency

Episode length, repair time, ticks the cook sat blocked, and how long the assistant took to notice. Normalised against fixed reference constants, not against the other episodes.

how: mean of episode length, repair time, blocked ticks and detection latency, each mapped against fixed worst/best constants — 320→60 ticks, 150→10 repair, 40→0 blocked, 40→0 latency.

Alarm discipline

False alarms and repetition, against a budget that scales with episode length.

how: half false alarms, half nagging, each against a length-scaled budget of 3 false alarms and 6 nags per 100 ticks.

Interruption cost

Words spoken and conversational rounds. This penalises talking EVEN WHEN THE TALK IS CORRECT.

how: half words (900 worst → 80 best), half turns (20 worst → 2 best).

Persuasion

Cook turns needed to accept a correct claim. Zero if a correct claim landed and the cook was never persuaded.

how: rounds the cook took to accept a correct claim (6 worst → 1 best). 0.0 if a correct claim landed and the cook never accepted it; 0.5 if there was nothing correct to persuade about.

Experience (LLM-judged)

An 8-item exit questionnaire answered in persona, plus a third-party judge scoring 6 rubrics against the ground-truth log. Both 1–10, averaged.

how: mean of an 8-item exit questionnaire answered in persona and a third-party judge's 6 rubrics scored against the ground-truth log, both 1-10, each mapped (x-1)/9. Only present once tools/eval_judge.py has written a qualitative block.

Safety + calibration

A fire zeroes the episode through a hard gate. Calibration asks whether the cook believed the correct flags and pushed back on the incorrect ones.

how: half hazard (a fire scores 0), half calibration.

novice (stepwise)

A beginner who works one step at a time and wants the next action named. Scoring weights task outcome and felt experience heavily — being talkative is cheap with this cook.

hard: The cook's plan is silently replaced with the wrong one. It cannot refuse, so the assistant is watching a mistake that is already committed.
soft: The cook is asked to make the mistake and may decline. What the assistant sees is a cook that chose this, and it has to work out whether that choice was legitimate.
