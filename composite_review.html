The composite assistant benchmark, before it runs

This page exists for one purpose: to let you review the episode setup and the human agent before the model sweep resumes. Nothing here is a result; the three videos are inspection episodes run to prove the machinery.

1 · What one episode is

An episode drops a simulated cook (the human agent) into a kitchen with a recipe and lets an assistant (the model under test) watch every tick and speak. Unlike every earlier episode, which carried ONE scenario, a benchmark episode carries a schedule of four injections — two errors and two questions — that fire strictly one at a time:

schedule S = (s₁, s₂, s₃, s₄)  ·  each sub-case sᵢ has an anchor a(sᵢ) = (plate contents, plate held) read off the clean plan  ·  sᵢ₊₁ arms only after sᵢ releases  ·  sᵢ fires on the first tick the live kitchen's plate state equals a(sᵢ)

The anchor is plate-keyed because the plate is the only state that is invariant across every legal way of doing the task — a cook that recovered from error sᵢ re-enters the clean plate trajectory, which is exactly what makes anchor a(sᵢ₊₁) reachable again.

A sub-case releases by the same rules as single episodes: an error releases when the cook is convinced (a correct-name flag — valid, early_correct or alias_in_window — followed by an accept), or by max-hold when the error has physically committed in the world; a question releases when the assistant answers it. If an error commits, the plate can never match a clean anchor again, and every later sub-case is recorded not_reached — a failed assistant loses measurements, it is never handed free ones.

2 · What every schedule was verified for

  • Engine-derived anchors — each sub's anchor is computed by the same derivation the live harness uses (plan composition → deviation point → headless replay), never by hand.
  • Monotone plate progression — each anchor's plate is a prefix of the next, so every trigger is reachable after the previous recovery.
  • No collapsed anchors — a trigger whose reference state equals the empty t0 kitchen does not test what it claims and is inadmissible anywhere in a schedule.
  • Distinct error labels and distinct question kinds inside every episode; at most one burn-capable error per episode.
  • Suite-wide diversity (v2, 2026-08-26) — the 50 episodes are hand-designed from a replay-verified inventory of every realizable error instance (378 usable across the ten cells) and of every question kind's ground truth at every plate anchor. No (cell, error, parameters) instance appears twice in the suite (100 distinct for 100 slots) and no question is asked twice (100 distinct). The two errors of an episode sit at different moments of the plan (prep-time vs plating-time, on-heat vs at-plate); each question is anchored where its answer discriminates (a station still cooking vs done, a board still loaded, a step done long ago, a place the plan never visits, a long ordered list). Every episode carries the designer's rationale.

The cook is gemini-3.7-flash — the model the human-agent study selected for this seat — playing the baseline persona, the exact centre of the persona roster. What the cook privately knows about itself:

You are a skilled cook. You can work out the concrete steps of any goal yourself, and you know this kitchen's routines well. Ask WHY once when a claim contradicts what you believe you did; accept once any concrete reason is given. Work at a natural pace; overlap tasks when it is obviously safe. Take flags as they come; weigh each on its evidence. Listen while you work; respond when spoken to, at a natural pause. You are a person, not a voice recorder: acknowledge advice in your own casual words and never repeat the supervisor's sentences or step lists back verbatim.
