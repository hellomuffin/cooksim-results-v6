Does the new persona card still move the cook's behaviour?

This page introduces the persona-contrast check for statement bank v3. The instrument is the B axis of the human-agent probe suite: the same start state and the same assistant line are played to two cooks whose cards differ in exactly one axis, the other four at medium — and the card is the full five-axis card, exactly as in the sweep. One behavioural variable per family is read from the cook's JSON or the executor log, never from a judge; a pair agrees when the difference has the direction the axis predicts, and is separated when the two personas differed at all. Cook model: gemini-3.7-flash; two seeds per probe. The old bank is the one the 2026-08 sweep ran; bank v3 is the disposition bank adopted on 2026-08-27 (council page). The point to judge: with rules replaced by dispositions, does the contrast survive — and where it does not, is that the cook, the card, or a probe variable that was bound to the old scripted behaviour (B3 reads "objects to guessing" from cue words the old card used to hand the cook)?

1 · Agreement by family across the banks

Bank v3 is the first disposition bank (council rounds 1–2). Bank v3.1 is the adopted bank: council round 3 sharpened the four levels whose contrast had weakened — trust low/high and interruption 1/3 — by naming a first instinct or habit, still without a fixed move or phrasing; the other levels are unchanged (their v3 rollouts are reused). Two probe variables were also revised because they measured the old scripts, not the disposition: B2 now reads resistance as turns before accepting with a hard reject counting double (under every bank all cooks contradict a plainly false claim about their own hands, so "pushes back first" never separated), and B4 counts a first reply that asks for less interruption as maximally terse (a 19-word "keep the line clear unless there's a problem" is the interruption-3 behaviour, not chattiness). The old-bank and v3 columns are rescored with the revised variables so the comparison is like for like.
