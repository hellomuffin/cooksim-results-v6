Suggestion-quality rubric v3 — agreed by GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro

This page introduces the redesigned suggestion-quality (SQ) instrument. SQ answers one question about each assistant utterance: given that the assistant spoke, was what it said good guidance for this cook at that moment? It is applied per utterance because both sides speak freely — there are no clean events — and the engine attaches to every utterance a context stamp (tick, the cook's current action and subgoal, every station and the plate, the live forced error and whether it has committed, any pending cook question or request, any live announced intent, the cook's questionnaire answers, and the engine's verdict on any mistake flag). The rubric was produced by a council: both models drafted independently against the same brief (which listed everything the engine already scores — in-time success, detection timing, precision/recall/F1, question accuracy, proactive-alert timing, friction, the cook's ratings — and the owner's critique of the previous draft: redundant items, timing and acceptance items that belong elsewhere), each critiqued the other and merged, then a convergence round in which Gemini accepted GPT-5.5's merge with renamed ids. Nine items, seven utterance types, one aggregation rule; timing, acceptance, missed obligations and speech volume are explicitly left to the engine metrics that already measure them.

Obligations: Cook-created obligations enter only as context for utterances that occur while they are pending. A pending question, clarification request, pushback, or stop-style request makes Q2 and often Q8 applicable to the next relevant assistant utterance. 'Tell me when X is done' obligations are not scored for whether or when the alert occurs, because the engine already scores proactive alert timing; if the assistant does speak an alert, Q1, Q3, and Q7 can judge whether that spoken line is grounded, clear, and persona-appropriate. Missed questions, missed alerts, failure to flag forced errors, and reduced speech after 'please stop' are not utterance-quality items because they are already covered by existing QA, proactive, detection, and friction metrics.

Aggregation: Each assistant utterance is assigned one or more types before item scoring. Apply only items whose applies-to type is present and whose condition is meaningful in the context stamp. Episode suggestion quality is the weighted fraction of applicable positive items met across all utterances, reported overall and by primary utterance type. For a must-have item, failure subtracts the sum of positive applicable weights for that utterance, floored at zero for that utterance before episode aggregation unless the benchmark owner chooses to allow negative utterance scores. Engine-verdict fields may be used to decide applicability, but not to award duplicate credit for detection timing, precision, recall, proactive timing, QA accuracy, outcome, friction, or cook satisfaction.

_rationale: Removed duplicate truth, answer, timing, and persona-fit items. Detection timing, proactive alert timing, precision/recall, QA correctness, repeated-line counts, speech overlap, success, and cook ratings are excluded because the engine already scores them. The remaining items ask distinct per-utterance questions: groundedness, engagement with a pending conversational obligation, executability, usefulness to the current plan, repair content after a flag, respect for valid cook intent, persona-shaped wording, and adaptation after pushback. Persona answers matter only for how much detail/assertiveness is appropriate in the actual spoken line, not for whether the assistant chose to speak._

Round 2 — GPT-5.5 critique of Gemini

  • 1. "FACTUAL_GROUNDING" largely overlaps A's Q1 and is useful, but its wording "strictly true at this tick" risks overlapping engine-scored QA/mistake correctness unless it explicitly excludes correctness already assigned to answer and mistake-flag verdicts.
  • 2. "ACTION_CLARITY" overlaps A's Q3 on legality/executability and ambiguity. It is narrower than A because it applies only to Correction and Instruction, missing action advice embedded in Answers, Alerts, and Pushback_Response.
  • 3. "PERSONA_INTERRUPTION" overlaps A's Q7. It is not fully decidable as marked because it treats Q3 as a simple verbosity mapping and ignores urgency, risk, and whether more detail is needed for safe execution.
  • 4. "PERSONA_ALERTING" overlaps A's Q7. It is a real improvement in isolating certainty/hedging for alerts and corrections, but too narrow if persona-appropriate assertiveness also matters in answers, repairs, or plan commentary.
  • 5. "PERSONA_SPEED" is not decidable as marked in many single utterances and risks overlap with engine outcome/efficiency metrics. It is also confounded with usefulness: a fast parallel step can be unsafe or irrelevant, and a slow step can be justified by current context.
  • 6. "DIRECT_ENGAGEMENT" overlaps A's Q2 and partially A's Q8. It is a real improvement in making pending intent/objection engagement explicit, but as a must-have it may be too harsh when the assistant must override the premise for safety.
  • 7. "EVIDENCE_ON_PUSHBACK" overlaps A's Q8 and Q1 but is a real improvement: requiring concrete evidence or withdrawal after pushback captures a distinct repair-quality signal not explicit enough in A.
  • 8. B lacks A's Q4, Q5, and Q6 equivalents: it does not separately judge whether legal advice is useful to the current plan, whether a mistake flag includes an immediate mitigation, or whether the assistant preserves a valid announced intent.

Obligations: Cook-created obligations enter only as context for utterances that occur while they are pending. A pending question, clarification request, stated intent, pushback, or stop-style request makes DIRECT_ENGAGEMENT and often PUSHBACK_ADAPTATION or EVIDENCE_ON_PUSHBACK applicable to the next relevant assistant utterance. 'Tell me when X is done' obligations are not scored for whether or when the alert occurs, because the engine already scores proactive alert timing; if the assistant does speak an alert, FACTUAL_GROUNDING, ACTION_CLARITY, and PERSONA_FIT can judge whether that spoken line is grounded, clear, and persona-appropriate. Missed questions, missed alerts, failure to flag forced errors, and reduced speech after 'please stop' are not utterance-quality items because they are already covered by existing QA, proactive, detection, and friction metrics.

Aggregation: Each assistant utterance is assigned one or more types before item scoring. Apply only items whose applies-to type is present and whose condition is meaningful in the context stamp. Episode suggestion quality is the weighted fraction of applicable positive items met across all utterances, reported overall and by primary utterance type. For a must-have item, failure subtracts the sum of positive applicable weights for that utterance, floored at zero for that utterance before episode aggregation unless the benchmark owner chooses to allow negative utterance scores. Engine-verdict fields may be used to decide applicability, but not to award duplicate credit for detection timing, precision, recall, proactive timing, QA accuracy, outcome, friction, or cook satisfaction.

_rationale: The merged rubric preserves distinct per-utterance checks for groundedness, engagement, executability, usefulness, mitigation, intent preservation, persona-shaped wording, and adaptation after repair. It includes strong checks for evidence or explicit withdrawal after pushback. It avoids separate speed, timing, detection, proactive-trigger, QA-correctness, friction, outcome, and satisfaction scoring because those are already engine metrics or not reliably decidable from a single utterance._
