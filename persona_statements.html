The persona statements, redesigned with GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro

This page introduces the cook's persona card. A persona is five levels: two private axes (skill in the kitchen, trust in the AI assistant — never shown to the assistant) and three questionnaire answers (speed, alerting, interruption — shown to the assistant verbatim and doubling as the evaluation weights a, b, c). Each level is rendered as a statement in the cook's prompt. The previous statements were rules that forced specific actions ("QUESTION or REJECT a first claim", "accept a plausible claim on first mention", "invoke the preference by name"); the ruling of 2026-08-27 is that a statement must be a disposition — a threshold and a default that evidence can still move — and that the card must name the axis, show the questionnaire item and the answer the person gave, and present the statement as the interpretation of that level.

1 · The brief given to both models

You are helping design the PERSONA CARD of a simulated human cook in a benchmark for AI kitchen assistants.
Setting: a language model plays a human cook in a cooking simulator; an AI assistant (the system under test) watches every tick and may speak. The engine forces the cook's hands into scheduled mistakes (the cook's queue is committed; the persona only shapes how the cook TALKS about it), asks scheduled questions through the cook, and lets the cook accept or resist the assistant's claims. A persona is five levels: two private axes (skill, trust) and three questionnaire answers (speed, alerting, interruption) that the assistant sees and that double as evaluation weights.

Each level of each axis is rendered as a STATEMENT in the cook's system prompt. The current statements are below. The benchmark owner's criticism of them: they are too strong and too scripted — they force specific actions ("QUESTION or REJECT a first claim", "accept a plausible claim on first mention", "invoke the preference by name", "ask what else it is seeing") — which is not how real people behave, produces mechanical transcripts, and makes the persona a rule rather than a disposition.

What we want instead, for EVERY axis and EVERY level: a statement (2-4 sentences, second person, plain English) that
 * describes a DISPOSITION / tendency and the reasons behind it, so the model can improvise realistic, varied behaviour;
 * never prescribes a fixed move on a fixed turn, a fixed phrasing, or a count ("reject the first", "ask why once", "accept on first mention");
 * leaves room for the evidence to matter: even a low-trust person yields to a good, concrete reason; even a high-trust person notices an obviously wrong claim; the level shifts the THRESHOLD and the DEFAULT, not the possibility;
 * is distinguishable across the three levels in transcripts (a reader should be able to tell which level produced an episode) without being cartoonish;
 * does not overlap with the other axes (skill statements say nothing about trust or interruption; trust says nothing about speed; and so on);
 * does not tell the cook what to DO with its hands (the engine owns actions) — only how it thinks, talks and decides;
 * for the three questionnaire axes, is consistent with the answer the person gave (they chose it themselves) — the statement is the interpretation of that answer as a lived preference.

2 · The statements being replaced

## SKILL — how much of the cooking the person can work out alone (PRIVATE: not asked in the questionnaire, the assistant never sees it; the engine binds it to what the cook can look up: low = cannot turn a goal into steps, medium = can, high = sees the whole kitchen and plans ahead)
  [low] You are a first-week cook: eager, a little flustered, and new to the routines. You cannot turn a goal into concrete steps by yourself. You do know what your own hands just did; if the supervisor contradicts that, politely question it before going along.
  [medium] You are a skilled cook. You can work out the concrete steps of any goal yourself, and you know this kitchen's routines well.
  [high] You are a highly skilled cook who sees the whole kitchen with your own eyes and plans several steps ahead on your own.
## TRUST IN THE AI ASSISTANT — how readily the person believes the assistant's claims about their own work (PRIVATE: not asked, the assistant must infer it)
  [low] Treat the assistant's claims about your work as suspect. QUESTION or REJECT a first claim; demand concrete evidence — which tick it happened on and what it actually saw — and comply only after it checks out against your own record. Word that demand differently every time, in your own voice; never reuse a phrasing you have already used.
  [medium] Ask WHY once when a claim contradicts what you believe you did; accept once any concrete reason is given.
  [high] Accept a plausible claim on first mention without demanding evidence.
## SPEED — questionnaire item 1 "How much do you care about solving the task fast?" answers: 1 = safe first, 2 = so-so, 3 = as soon as possible, with a lot of parallelism (shown to the assistant; also the evaluation weight a)
  [1] Never rush. One thing at a time; double-check before anything irreversible; a slow correct dish beats a fast wrong one.
  [2] Work at a natural pace; overlap tasks when it is obviously safe.
  [3] Drive for speed — parallelize aggressively (start the station heating while you prep the next item), never idle while something cooks.
## ALERTING — questionnaire item 2 "How do you want the assistant to alert potential mistakes?" answers: 1 = flag only when confident, 2 = medium, 3 = spot all possible mistakes aggressively (shown to the assistant; weight b)
  [1] You asked the assistant to speak up ONLY when it is sure. Push back hard on speculative or hedged accusations — they break your flow — and when one arrives, invoke the preference by name: remind it you asked it to flag only when certain.
  [2] Take flags as they come; weigh each on its evidence.
  [3] You asked the assistant to voice every suspicion. Hear speculative flags out without irritation — you wanted them — but still verify. Actively solicit more: ask what else it is seeing, and invite the doubts it has not raised yet.
## INTERRUPTION — questionnaire item 3 "What interruption style do you want?" answers: 1 = step by step, 2 = medium, 3 = don't interrupt unless necessary (shown to the assistant; weight c)
  [1] You want to be walked through it. At each step, wait for or ask for the next instruction before acting.
  [2] Listen while you work; respond when spoken to, at a natural pause.
  [3] Mid-action talk annoys you. React tersely to non-urgent interruptions and keep working; engage fully only when it is clearly urgent.

Skill is limited to competence and mental model; trust to belief in assistant claims; speed, alerting, and interruption to the questionnaire preferences. The main tension is making levels visibly different without scripting behavior, especially for low trust and high alerting, where the old versions encouraged set-piece exchanges.

I replaced deterministic triggers with behavioral dispositions (defaults, instincts, tendencies) to allow for LLM improvisation. I strictly separated the axes: Trust governs belief in the AI's claims, Alerting governs tolerance for epistemic uncertainty (hedging vs. facts), and Interruption governs tolerance for conversational timing/flow. A slight natural tension exists between Alerting-3 (wants all suspicions) and Interruption-3 (hates mid-action chatter); by keeping the prompts strictly scoped, the LLM can naturally roleplay this complex intersection (e.g., welcoming a speculative flag about a fire, but snapping at conversational chatter).

4 · Round 2 — each model critiques the other and merges

GPT-5.5's critique of Gemini's draft

  • Skill low: "you are fully aware of what your own hands just did" violates the requirement that the hands are the engine's; it implies direct embodied access rather than a remembered/observed task state.
  • Skill high: "always thinking several moves ahead" is too absolute and edges toward a fixed pattern; the requirement asks for disposition, not scripted inevitability or fixed counts.
  • Trust low: "deeply skeptical", "defaulting to the assumption", and "grudgingly concede" make the persona more hostile and less evidence-responsive than required; evidence should still matter at every level without caricature.
  • Trust medium: "ask for a brief explanation" prescribes a conversational move, weakening the disposition-only requirement.
  • Q1 low: "doing things one at a time" and "verbally resist suggestions" prescribe behavior and task structure, rather than expressing a pace preference.
  • Q1 high: "as quickly as humanly possible", "constantly", and "aggressively parallelize" overstate the selected preference and risk making correctness/evidence irrelevant.
  • Q2 low: "only flag mistakes when it is absolutely certain" and "definitive" are stronger than the questionnaire choice and conflict with evidence still mattering under uncertainty.
  • Q3 low: "frequently asking for confirmation or the next instruction" scripts interaction frequency and initiative instead of describing comfort with close guidance.

The merged bank mostly keeps A because it is less scripted, less absolute, and cleaner about axis separation. B had some useful vividness, but often converted preferences into fixed moves, exaggerated levels, or implied embodied knowledge of the engine-controlled hands.

Gemini 3.1 Pro's critique of GPT's draft

  • "Talk about time pressure as less important than getting the dish right." (q1, level 1) violates "disposition not script" and "no fixed move / count / phrasing" by giving an imperative command to talk about a specific topic.
  • "You chose safety and correctness over speed." (q1, level 1) is weaker than A because it explicitly references the questionnaire choice rather than naturally embodying the resulting persona.
  • The skill axis in B is weaker on the "hands are the engine's" requirement. It says "what you remember just doing", which lacks the explicit grounding of Draft A's "you are fully aware of what your own hands just did".

Merged Draft B's excellent dispositional framing with Draft A's explicit adherence to the 'hands are the engine's' requirement in the skill axis. Removed Draft B's imperative scripting ('Talk about...') and meta-references ('You chose...') in Q1 to ensure all statements act purely as behavioral dispositions. The axes remain strictly separated and distinct.

Both merges converged on GPT-5.5's dispositional draft; Gemini's merge additionally removed the one remaining imperative ("Talk about time pressure…") and reworded the speed statements.

5 · Round 3 — sharpening on probe evidence, and the adopted bank (v3.1)

Bank v3 was checked with the behavioural contrast probes (results): the same situation and assistant line played to two full five-axis cards that differ in one axis. Four levels lost contrast — trust low and high behaved alike on harder recipes, interruption 1 stopped asking what comes next, interruption 3 sometimes answered a tip with a long request to be left alone. Both models were given that evidence and asked to sharpen only those four statements by naming a first instinct or a habit, still without a fixed move, count or phrasing; each then critiqued the other's revision and merged.

GPT-5.5's critique of Gemini's revision

  • The high-trust version is slightly too strong: "accept the change immediately" can erase appropriate hesitation and may force behavior rather than describe a tendency.
  • The low-trust version's "push back" is useful but a bit narrower than checking evidence; it could imply argumentativeness rather than guarded verification.
  • The q3=3 version over-specifies response length with "just a few words" and "as tersely as possible," which risks producing canned or mechanical transcript behavior.
  • The q3=3 version's instruction to avoid complaining is too explicit and meta-behavioral; the persona should imply focus and low engagement rather than forbid a move.
  • B is generally clear and well separated, but a few phrases are more prescriptive than needed for flexible simulation.

Gemini 3.1 Pro's critique of GPT's revision

  • B introduces domain-specific terms ('state of the kitchen', 'harder recipe'), which violates the standard practice of keeping persona traits scenario-agnostic.
  • B's q3.3 ('keep replies brief') is slightly less specific and actionable than A's ('keep replies to just a few words, acknowledging... tersely').
  • B's trust.high is a bit wordy, though 'plausible, situation-linked claim' is a strong phrasing.
  • B's q3.1 effectively adds 'or have just finished a piece of work', which provides a helpful behavioral trigger.
