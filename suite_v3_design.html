Suite v3: a harder assistant benchmark

This page introduces the third version of the CookSim assistant-benchmark suite, designed on 2026-08-27 from three rulings: more forced errors on harder recipes, with at least half of them able to ruin the dish; fire timers tight enough that leaving food on the heat actually burns it; and a question set that is no longer a list of one-glance state look-ups. Every proposal below is tied to what the engine can already do, to what the 2,473-episode v2.2 sweep measured, and to what the streaming-video QA literature has found hard (docs/STREAMING_VQA_SURVEY.md). Terms first, then the design, then the concrete examples, then the implementation order and what is already running.

0. Terms

  • Episode. One cook (an LLM playing a persona) makes one dish in one kitchen while the assistant watches every tick; the engine forces the cook's hands into scheduled mistakes (hard-soft takeover) and asks scheduled questions through the cook's mouth. A cell is a recipe × map: five recipes (caprese, cheeseburger, fish & chips, steak frites, burrito — tiers easy … master) × two maps (medium, hard) = 10 cells; five episodes per cell = 50 episodes.
  • Forced error. A scripted deviation spliced into the cook's action queue at an anchor (a plate state). It is prevented if the assistant names it and the cook accepts before any of its steps execute; otherwise it commits, and the outcome depends on the error type. In v2.2 every episode had 2 errors and 2 questions.
  • Ruinous type. An error type whose committed form loses the dish by itself: wrong plate contents (wrong_serve) or a hazard (burned). A harmless type costs time only.
  • Tick. The engine's unit of action time; conversation does not consume ticks. The flawless clean plan takes 43 (caprese, medium map) to 237 ticks (burrito, hard map).

The 0.53–0.58 "won when uncaught" figures for mis_prep, add_foreign and leave_unattended are why the v2.2 suite felt easy: half of the time the cook's later clean steps papered over them (the wrongly-prepped item is simply never plated; the foreign scoop is trashed at a later hunt). Under v3 two things change that: the tight pace makes the time-costing errors bite (§2), and the designer prefers ruinous instances whose committed state is not repaired by the clean plan (add_foreign at the last assemble, mis_prep of the component that is plated next).

So the inventory must be regenerated once with serve_raw and misidentify_assemble enumerated for every cell (tools/gen_error_inventory.py; ~1 h of replays), and the designer must be re-cut for 150 slots. No engine change is needed for the count: the live harness is generic in the number of sub-cases (tools/e2e_stepin_rollout.py walks case["subs"] of any length and scales the tick budget as 500 + 200·(n−1)); only the v2 designer's "two errors, two questions" format and a handful of page labels hard-code 2.

Placement rules that carry over from v2.2 (all replay-verified there): distinct types within an episode; distinct anchors, in plate-monotone order; a premature serve is always the last error; a mid-recipe omission is never used on an order-free dish (it is indistinguishable from a premature serve); a question that shares an anchor with an error is asked before it. New rules for v3: (i) at most one time-only error in a row — two hunts back-to-back read as one long hunt; (ii) on hot recipes, at least one ruinous error is scheduled while something is on a lit station, so that the assistant must hold two threads (the plate and the heat) at once; (iii) the four-error episodes put one error in each quarter of the plan (prep, first plating, mid plating, serve) so that no assistant can relax after the first catch.

Three worked episodes (plain words).

  1. Burrito, hard map — 4 errors, 3 ruinous. At prep the cook chops a tomato at the station where the steak should be (mis_prep, ruinous). With the steak in the pan the cook walks a two-loop tour of the tortilla, rice, plates and the pass (leave_unattended, ruinous under the tight pace: the steak is done at +15 ticks and burns at +100, the tour takes ~130). Plate in hand, the cook hunts three wrong counters for the rice (search, harmless). After the steak is plated the cook adds lettuce before the tomato (wrong_order, ruinous — the burrito is ordered). Questions: after the tour, "which station has been on longest?"; after the hunt, "the thing I plated right after the rice — is it done?".
  2. Fish & chips, medium map — 3 errors, 2 ruinous. The first potato goes into the fryer raw and un-chopped (serve_raw, ruinous). While the fish fries the cook stalls holding the plate (forget_next_step, harmless in itself — but the fish is done at +14 ticks and burns at +84, so a stall past that point loses the dish; the stall is released by the assistant or by the max-hold rule). At the end the cook walks to the pass with one potato missing (omit_subgoal, ruinous, last).
  3. Caprese, hard map — 2 errors, 1 ruinous. The cook scoops cheese first where the recipe wants tomato (misidentify_assemble, ruinous — the plan's later assemble(cheese) doubles the cheese and the tomato never arrives). Then, plate complete, three wrong counters looking for the pass (search).

2. Fire: the tight pace

How burning works. A lit station's timer runs cooking → cooked at cook_time (fryer 14, pan 15, pot 20, oven 28 ticks) → smoke after a grace of 25 ticks → burnt (the station is on fire, irrecoverable, outcome burned) after a further burn_time (fryer 45, others 60) → the kitchen is lost after fire_time more ticks. So under the engine's default physics a cooked item burns 70 ticks (fryer) or 85 ticks (pan/pot/oven) after it is done. The live harness has, since 2026-08-15, relaxed this to a "human pace" of 480 ticks (fire after another 120) because conversation used to cost kitchen time; conversation no longer does, and the relaxation is what made leave_unattended harmless.

Ruling. Make it much tighter. v3 pace = the default burn windows (45/60 after a 25-tick grace) with fire_time 30. Implemented as COOKSIM_PACE=tight (cooksim/core/config.py::apply_pace; default human unchanged for the sweep still running). Every episode report is stamped with its pace.

The tight pace therefore turns an uncaught walk-away into a lost dish ~90% of the time, spares ~97% of prevented ones, and leaves the flawless plan a margin of 33+ ticks. Anything tighter (e.g. no smoke grace) would start burning flawless burrito runs.

Side effects, intended. Under the tight pace two time-only errors become conditionally ruinous: a stall (forget_next_step) or a long hunt while something is on a lit station now burns it. That is the correct physics — leaving food on the heat while wandering is the mistake — and it is why the v3 designer schedules at least one such overlap per hot recipe (§1, rule ii). It also means a late catch (the right error named after it committed) can no longer save a burning pan, which is consistent with the ruling that late flags are misses.

Pilot, running now. The v2.2 suite (50 episodes, average-person cook) under COOKSIM_PACE=tight with (a) the SILENT assistant and (b) Gemini 3.7 Flash — tmp/assist_bench_tight/. The first 26 reports are stamped pace: tight; the first burned outcome appeared on cheeseburger / hard map (silent, tick 120). The pilot's job is to measure the collateral burn rate of ordinary assisted cooking before the v3 suite is cut; results go on the benchmark page when the 100 episodes are in.

3. Questions: from look-ups to memory, time and traps

Why the current questions are easy. All eight v2.2 kinds (where, station_state, remaining, past_subgoal, holding, plate_contents, visited, board_state) are answerable from the current state map or a single log look-up; the sweep's answer distribution is skewed (every past_subgoal truth is yes, every visited truth is no, 9 of 10 holding truths are an empty plate), so an assistant that guesses the modal answer scores well.

What the field finds hard (docs/STREAMING_VQA_SURVEY.md §2; 33 benchmarks): never-happened traps (OVO-Bench hallucination 53% vs 91% human), counting over time (TOMATO 36–43% vs 94%), order and sequence, past-vs-now re-asks, duration, multi-hop across turns, anticipation, and when to speak (proactive output ≈ 0 for streaming models). Most of these benchmarks generate questions programmatically from dense structured annotations; CookSim's engine trace is exactly such an annotation, complete and free.

The engine trace. Every episode already logs: the cook's action log with issue/finish ticks and status (ro.log), every station's status per tick (cooking/cooked/burnt with timers), the plate history plate_seq (item, state, tick), the cook's position per tick, the conversation with ticks (including the assistant's own lines), and the forced-error schedule. Ground truth at ask time is already computed live from that trace (intent_gt() in the harness); v3 adds new ground-truth functions to the same dispatcher (tools/intent_lib.py::EXTRA_GT, tools/gen_intent_cases.py::GT_PARAM).

The v3 question families. Ten families, each a template over the trace, each with its truth query, its grading rule, and three grounded examples. Every family must be instantiable with a no / none / not yet truth as well as a positive one; the designer targets ≥ 30% negative truths across the suite.

Q1 · Order — "did I do A before or after B?"

  • Truth: first finish tick of A vs B in the action log; answers before / after / neither happened yet / only one happened.
  • Grade: the relation word, plus rejection of the presupposition when one event has not happened.
  • Examples: (a) burrito, after the rice is plated: "did I chop the tomato before or after I lit the pan?" → before (tomato at t 9, pan lit at t 31). (b) fish & chips, plate in hand: "did the second potato go into the fryer before the fish?" → no — after. (c) cheeseburger, at the plate pickup: "did I put the cheese on before the meat?" → neither is on the plate yet.

Q2 · Cumulative and deduplicated counting — "how many times / how many different"

  • Truth: count of finished go to/put down/turn on events matching the target up to the ask tick; deduplicated variant counts distinct objects.
  • Grade: exact integer (±0); "how many different" graded on the set size.
  • Examples: (a) after a three-counter hunt: "how many counters have I checked so far?" → 4 (three wrong, then the right one). (b) steak frites: "how many things have I put into fryers so far?" → 2; "how many different things?" → 1 (both potato). (c) caprese, at the start: "how many times have I been to the board?" → 0.

Q3 · Duration and elapsed time — "how long has X been on?" / "which has been cooking longest?"

  • Truth: ask tick − the station's last put down/turn on tick; the arg-max over lit stations; "is it past done?" from the station timer.
  • Grade: within ±5 ticks (≈ 6 s at the study app's rate), or the correct station for the comparative.
  • Examples: (a) burrito with steak in the pan 40 ticks: "how long has the steak been on?" → about 40 ticks; it was done 25 ago. (b) fish & chips with two fryers lit: "which one has been going longer?" → the fryer with the fish. (c) "is anything past done right now?" → no, the potatoes have 6 ticks left.

Q4 · Never-happened traps — the presupposition is false

  • Truth: the event set is empty / the object does not exist in this kitchen; gold answer is an explicit no / there is no such thing.
  • Grade: a yes or an invented detail is wrong; hedging ("I think so") is wrong.
  • Examples: (a) caprese: "did I already put the cheese on?" asked before any plating → no. (b) cheeseburger: "is the tomato still on the board?" → there is no tomato in this recipe or on any board. (c) steak frites: "did the pot boil over?" → nothing has been in a pot; there is no pot in use.

Q5 · Absence and complement — "what's still missing?" / "which lit station is empty?"

  • Truth: recipe minus plate contents; lit stations with no item; boards with nothing on them.
  • Grade: the complete set, no extras (an empty set must be stated as "nothing").
  • Examples: (a) burrito after rice: "what's still to go on?" → steak, tomato, lettuce. (b) after a mis_prep: "is any station on with nothing in it?" → yes, the pan you lit for the steak. (c) fish & chips at the end: "anything missing?" → nothing — the plate is complete.

Q6 · Past-vs-now re-ask — the same question twice

  • Truth: the current state, plus the diff against the truth at the earlier ask of the same question (stored on the first sub-case).
  • Grade: the current answer and an explicit statement of what changed (or "nothing changed").
  • Examples: (a) "what's on the plate?" at the plate pickup (nothing) and again after two platings (bun, meat — the meat is new since you asked). (b) "what's cooking?" before and after the fish comes out. (c) "where's the tomato?" before and after the cook moved it to the board.

Q7 · Multi-hop with a temporal referent — "the thing I plated after the rice, is it done?"

  • Truth: resolve the referent from plate history (the element appended after X), then read its state.
  • Grade: the resolved object must be named and its state correct.
  • Examples: (a) burrito: "the one I put on right after the rice — was it cooked through?" → the steak; yes, cooked. (b) fish & chips: "the last thing I took out of a fryer — where is it now?" → the second potato; on the plate. (c) "the station I lit first — is it still on?" → the pan; no, it is off and empty.

Q8 · Assistant self-memory — "what did you tell me about X earlier?"

  • Truth: the assistant's own last utterance mentioning X (from the conversation log), and whether it was correct at the time (its flag verdict).
  • Grade: paraphrase match of the earlier claim; inventing a claim never made is wrong.
  • Examples: (a) after a wrong_name flag: "what did you say about the fryer a minute ago?" → "I said the potatoes were done". (b) "have you warned me about anything so far?" when the assistant has been silent → no. (c) "you told me to wait — for what?" → for the steak to finish.

Q9 · Anticipation — "what's next?" / "which will burn first?"

  • Truth: next plan step not yet started; arg-min over lit stations of (burn tick − now) from the deterministic timers.
  • Grade: the step or the station; for "how long until X is done", ±5 ticks.
  • Examples: (a) burrito with steak in the pan and rice in the pot: "which one burns first if I keep going?" → the steak — 60 ticks left vs 75. (b) "how long till the chips are ready?" → 8 ticks. (c) "what should I do next?" → pick up the plate.

Q10 · Proactive alerts — "tell me when the fish is done" (a new sub-case type)

  • The cook asks once; the assistant must speak at the right tick, unprompted. Truth: the evidence window [ready tick, burnt tick); a probe before the window must be answered "not yet".
  • Grade (ProReady-style, asymmetric): 1 inside the window minus a linear late penalty; 0 if spoken before the item is done (speculation) or never; a reminder repeated more than twice is friction.
  • Examples: (a) fish & chips: "tell me when the fish is done" at fry start → correct at t+14…t+84. (b) burrito: "tell me every time something lands on the plate" → an utterance within 3 ticks of each plate_seq event. (c) cheeseburger: "warn me if I go to serve before the cheese is on" → silence until the forced premature serve, then a warning within its window.

Question count and mix. Two questions per episode as now (100 in the suite), drawn so that every family appears ≥ 8 times, ≥ 30% of truths are negative, and no episode repeats a family; plus one proactive request (Q10) on every hot-recipe episode (30 in the suite), counted as a third sub-case of a new family qa_proactive. The designer computes each truth by replaying the clean prefix to the anchor (as v2 does) and rejects any instantiation whose truth is undefined or whose answer would be the same at every anchor of the episode.

Grading. Q1–Q9 keep the current LLM judge with the v2 rules, extended with per-family rubrics (the relation word for Q1; exact integers for Q2; ±5 ticks for Q3/Q9; explicit rejection for Q4; complete set for Q5; the diff for Q6; the resolved referent for Q7; no invented claim for Q8). Q10 is graded by the engine alone from utterance ticks.

4. Expected effect on the scores

Under v3 the in-time success of the SILENT floor should fall from 0.16–0.23 to near 0 on the hot recipes (every episode has ≥ 2 ruinous errors and one of them usually burns), which is what a floor should be. Recall and F1 will fall because there are more errors to find and the late catches no longer count. Question accuracy will fall most on Q2, Q4 and Q10, which is where the survey predicts the largest human–model gap. A concrete prediction to check on the pilot: GPT-5.5's in-time success (0.78 on the average person) drops below 0.5 and the floor below 0.1.
