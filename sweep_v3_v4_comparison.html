Sweep v3 vs sweep v4 — the same benchmark under the revised harness

built 2026-09-08 15:53 UTC · v3 root tmp/assist_bench_v3 (1347 complete episodes) · v4 root tmp/assist_bench_v4 (1326 complete episodes; engine stamps 4d15175f2d: 959, None: 329, 4d23961171: 38)

0 · What is being compared

Both sweeps run the same fifty suite-v3 episodes (two to four forced errors each, at least half ruinous, tight fire timers, questions from families Q1–Q9), with the same nine assistants watching the same three simulated cooks (Gemini 3.7 Flash plays the cook) through rendered frames. Sweep v3 ran on 2026-08-27; sweep v4 ran on 2026-09-05 after the harness rulings of that week. The two sweeps are therefore a study of the harness, holding the tasks and the models fixed:

  • Cook prompt — restructured into sections; the kitchen's rules are stated; the plan and subgoal list are withheld; WHAT YOU KNOW is the goal, the cook's own announced change, and its past actions.
  • Assistant prompt — no longer shows the cook's public plan.
  • Recipes — every benchmark recipe is plating-order strict (two identical items remain interchangeable).
  • Injected-error task — split into a belief framing and an attention framing, each carrying an engine-hint line that states whether the assistant's flag is correct; two trust levels (high: the first correct warning; low: verified in the cook's own state map).
  • Assistant latency — a reply that took x seconds arrives ceil(x / 1.2) ticks later while the cook keeps working; one call in flight at a time.
  • leave_unattended — injected only while something is on the heat.
  • Question sub-cases (patch during v4) — a question sub-case ends when the question is asked; the answer is captured when it arrives. Before the patch the sub-case held until the answer, and the charged latency let the cook run past the next error's anchor.
Read v4 with one caveat. During the v4 sweep an interaction between the charged latency and the question sub-cases was found: a question sub-case held until the answer arrived, and because the cook keeps working during that hold, the next error's anchor was passed in many short episodes, so the error fired late or never. The harness was patched (question sub-cases end at the ask) and every affected episode is being re-run under the patch; until that finishes, the 'errors fired' column shows how much of each cell is still affected. The v3 sweep had no latency and did not have this problem.

1 · Definitions

For one episode with reference length Tref, cap Tcap = Tref + allowance, and k forced errors:

S = 1[dish served correctly and T ≤ Tcap]   (in-time success)
R = tp / kfired,   P = tp / (tp + fp)   (an error counts as detected when the assistant names it, correctly, inside its window)
DT = mean over fired errors of {1 if flagged before the error committed, ½ if flagged after commit but before release, 0 otherwise}
Eff = (a·TT + c·CC) / (a + c) on won dishes, TT = Tref / T, CC = 1 − interruption share
AQ = mean over assistant rounds of the weighted share of rubric items met (0 when a must-item fails), graded by a flash-tier judge from the other vendor
QA = share of question answers the judge marks correct;   y = ⌈ x / 1.2 ⌉ ticks of charged latency for a reply that took x seconds (v4 only)

Score columns pool over the episodes in which at least one error fired (gate g2), which is the convention of the suite page; errors fired is the share of planned errors that actually fired and is reported on every row because it is exactly what the v4 defect changes. Δ is v4 − v3 with a normal 95% confidence interval on the difference of episode means; the assistant order follows S in v4.

Rank agreement between the sweeps (Spearman ρ over the nine assistants): on S 0.45; on AQ 0.90.

How to read it. The v4 harness is harder for everyone: the cook no longer carries a plan, every plate must be built in order, and a slow reply now costs game time, so in-time success falls across the board and the timeout share rises most for the assistants with the longest replies. Detection recall moves less than success does, which says the assistants see the errors about as well as before but their warnings arrive later relative to a cook who keeps moving. Where the two sweeps disagree on order, the latency column is usually the reason.
