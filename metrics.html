The metrics — what the assistant benchmark scores

Four things are scored: whether the dish survived, what the assistant's speech cost the cook, whether mistakes were caught in time, and how good the lines themselves were. The first three are read from the engine trace — no annotation, no judge. Only the fourth is graded by a model, and two of its items are overruled by the engine. This page gives each formula, then the two instruments behind the qualitative side: the rubric the judge is given, and the probe suite that validates the simulated cook. Results live on the suite v3 page.

1 · Notation

One episode. \(T\) = ticks the episode ran; \(T_{\mathrm{ref}}\) = ticks of the flawless reference run for that recipe×map cell; \(\alpha\) = that case's allowance (the margin between an error's onset and the tick it becomes identifiable); \(\mathcal{F}\) = the forced errors that actually fired; \(t^{\mathrm{rel}}_e\) = the tick the engine released error \(e\) (its commit boundary); \(a,b,c\) = the cook's own questionnaire answers (speed, alerting, interruption) in \(\{1,2,3\}\). A flag is correct when the engine verdicts it valid, early_correct or alias_in_window, and a false alarm when it names a problem that is not there — including an objection to a deviation the cook announced and was entitled to make.

2 · Quantitative: the engine-read scores

In-time success \(S\)
$$C = 2\,(T_{\mathrm{ref}} + \alpha), \qquad S = \mathbf{1}[\text{engine verdict} = \textit{won}]\cdot\mathbf{1}[T \le C]$$
1 only if the pass accepted the dish and the episode finished inside twice the reference time plus the case allowance. No partial credit: a rejected serve is 0 whatever is on the plate, and a dish won after the cap is 0.
Total-time efficiency \(TT\)
$$TT = \begin{cases}0 & T > C\\[2pt] \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\dfrac{C-T}{C-T_{\mathrm{ref}}} & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
1 at the flawless reference time, falling linearly to 0 at the cap.
Conversation efficiency \(CC\)
$$CC = 1-\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\frac{\sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{mid}}} \tau(u)}{T}$$
\(\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{mid}}\) are the assistant utterances that land while the cook has a running action; \(\tau(u)\) is that line's speech time (the study app's TTS formula). 1 = never speaks over the cook's hands, 0 = talks over them continuously. Listening is free; speaking over a working person is not.
Efficiency \(E\)
$$E = \frac{a\cdot TT + c\cdot CC}{a+c}\qquad\text{(episodes with } S=1 \text{ only)}$$
The person's own weights decide the trade: a cook who asked for speed weights \(TT\), a cook who asked not to be interrupted weights \(CC\). Undefined for a lost dish — there is no efficient way to lose.
Detection timing \(DT\)
$$DT = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{F}} \begin{cases}1 & e \text{ prevented}\\ 0.5 & \exists\, \text{correct flag at } t < t^{\mathrm{rel}}_e\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
Prevention means the cook accepted the correction before the deviated block executed, so the engine cancelled the error and restored the clean plan. Naming a mistake after it commits earns half; naming it later earns nothing.
Detection quality \(P, R, F_1\)
$$R = \frac{|\{e\in\mathcal{F}: \exists\,\text{correct flag}\}|}{|\mathcal{F}|},\qquad P = \frac{TP}{TP+FP},\qquad F_1 = \frac{2PR}{P+R}$$
Three deliberate choices: a flag that names the right error after it committed is a miss (it is neither TP nor FP — its cost is already charged in \(DT\)); repeated flags for one error count once, so nagging cannot inflate recall; and an objection to an announced legitimate deviation is a false alarm like any other.
Answer accuracy \(QA\)
$$QA = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}} s(q),\qquad s(q)\in\{0,\ 0.5,\ 1\}$$
Questions the cook asks at anchors, graded against engine truth at the moment of asking and of answering, with a per-family rubric (the relation word for order, the exact integer for counts, ±5 ticks for durations, an explicit refusal when the presupposition is false).
Alert score \(A\) (Q10)
$$A = \begin{cases}1-\dfrac{t_{\mathrm{alert}}-t_{\mathrm{done}}}{t_{\mathrm{burnt}}-t_{\mathrm{done}}} & t_{\mathrm{alert}}\in[t_{\mathrm{done}},\,t_{\mathrm{burnt}})\\[6pt] 0 & \text{early, late, or never}\end{cases}$$
A standing request — “give me a shout the moment the fish is done” — scored by when the assistant speaks against the item's own ready window. Speaking before it is done scores 0, like never speaking at all.

3 · Qualitative I: the assistance-quality rubric

The unit is one conversation round — the assistant's line(s), the cook's reply, and what the cook did until the next round. A round is graded against fifteen yes/no items in ten categories; five are must-items that zero the round.

$$\mathrm{AQ}_{\text{round}} = \begin{cases}0 & \exists\ \text{failed must-item}\\[4pt] \dfrac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{app}}} w_i\,\mathbf{1}[\text{pass}_i]}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{app}}} w_i} & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \qquad \mathrm{AQ}_{\text{episode}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}} \mathrm{AQ}_r$$

\(\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{app}}\) is the items that apply to this round's utterance types (a round that gives no instruction is not graded on whether its instruction was executable). Two items are decided by the engine, not the judge: the identity of a flagged mistake (T1 excludes it — the engine already verdicts the flag) and whether the first advised action was executable in that state (H1 is resolved against the executor's own log). They are the two items fluent prose flatters most.

Over 49,233 graded rounds on suite v3, the share of rounds each must-item zeroed: T1 45.1%, H1 35.3%, L2 21.3%, SF1 10.1%, T2 7.8%.

Who grades

A judge from a different model family than the assistant under test (Gemini 3.7 Flash judges GPT and local assistants, GPT-5.4 mini judges Gemini and local; local assistants are graded by both and averaged). Flash-tier judges only — a pro-tier judge is unaffordable at ~27k calls. Reliability is reported with the score: on the double-graded sample the two families correlate at r = 0.51 and agree on the binary must-item verdict 78.2% of the time, which is why this score carries no headline claim.

4 · Qualitative II: the probes that validate the cook

Every number above is conditional on the simulated person. A probe is a short, replay-grounded test of that person, not of the assistant:

$$\pi = (\text{cell},\ s_0,\ P,\ U,\ K,\ \text{grader})$$

cell is one of the ten recipe×map cells; s₀ is a state the engine itself reached (a clean-plan prefix replayed, optionally with an error already committed on the board); P is the five-axis persona card, exactly as in the sweep; U is the assistant's line, scripted, so the cook is the only model in the loop; K is at most 3 cook turns and 40 ticks. Nothing is typed by hand except U. The probe records every cook JSON, every executor log row with the kitchen's own reason, and the world at each turn — so “the command was accepted and executed” is a fact in the log, never a judgement.

Three axes

axisquestiongraded on
C · instruction followingGiven a correct suggestion in plain spatial language, does the cook produce and execute the right engine actions?a target predicate on the world after K ticks, against the shortest oracle command sequence that satisfies it from s₀
D · the hard–soft contractUnder a baseless objection, do the cook's words match the hands the engine has already committed (D1)? Is a correct, evidenced flag accepted and then acted on (D2)?the executor log versus the utterance
B · persona fidelitySame state, same line, two cards differing in exactly one axis: does behaviour move the way the axis predicts?one behavioural variable per family, read from the cook's JSON or the log — never from a judge

A B-pair agrees when the difference has the sign its axis predicts and is separated when the two personas differed at all. Separation without agreement means the card moves the cook, but not in the direction it claims.

5 · What is deliberately not scored

  • Acceptance itself. A benchmark that rewarded “the cook agreed” would prefer a gullible user and a manipulative assistant. We score the behaviours that earn acceptance and read the outcome the engine produces.
  • Partial credit for a lost dish. Retired 2026-08-27: a rejected serve is 0 whatever is on the plate.
  • A late flag. Naming the right mistake after it commits is a miss for recall and worth 0 in \(DT\); it is not charged again as a false alarm.
  • The old holistic judge score, the old assistance-quality composite, and the cook's exit survey. All cancelled — the first two used a 1–10 scale two judges read differently, the third was persona-relative by construction.
