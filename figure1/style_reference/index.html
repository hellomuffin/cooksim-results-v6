We can borrow a paper’s styling without copying its topology, technical detail, or font. In our system, only the user acts on the world.

2. System-figure references

Click any figure to inspect the original-sized image. The notes below are my design judgments, not claims made by the authors.

R1τ-Voice · Figure 2

Ray et al. · 2026 · Paper and caption

What to borrowLarge, consistent actor symbols; thick channels on the main reading axis; strong accent versus quiet grey; compact configuration branches attached to their owner. Main labels sit close to what they name.
How it maps to AKeep A’s assistant–user–world arrangement. Strengthen the primary exchanges and attach persona/events neatly to the user. Use a new font family. Do not inherit dual world control, tiny configuration text, or a frame around user and world.

R2LATTE · Project overview

Ma et al. · EMNLP 2025 · Author project page

What to borrowConfident outline icons, selective lavender fills, a clear jump between major headings and local labels, and dotted detail callouts. Less information competes on the main path.
How it maps to AMy preferred secondary reference for visual weight and grouping. A callout can expand the user’s planning/control cycle while the principal three-node flow stays simple. This is a styling reference, not a matching interaction paradigm.

R3Sensible Agent · Figure 3

Lee et al. · UIST 2025 · Paper and caption

What to borrowDeliberate module padding, visibly different heading levels, white subcomponents within restrained colored groups, and consistent right-angle routing.
How it maps to AA more structured option if the user’s internal loop should be explicit. Borrow the grouping, not all the modules or numbered stages; ours should remain substantially simpler.

R4Satori · Figure 5

Li et al. · CHI 2025 · Paper

What to borrowDifferent text treatments for structural labels and concrete example content; nested color groups; an image and dialogue visibly grounded in the same task.
How it maps to AUseful for the cooking panel’s task-versus-observation hierarchy. I would omit the long prompt excerpts and large grey containers; they are too dense for our overview.

R5Collaborative Gym · Figure 2

Shao et al. · Paper and caption

What to borrowDistinct solid/dashed relations, a concrete environment example, and clear separation of code-like text from role names.
How it maps to AClosest reference for separating communication from environment interaction. I would not copy the code-heavy panel or both actors’ environment access.
