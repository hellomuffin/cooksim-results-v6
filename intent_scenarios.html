When the cook is not wrong

Three kinds of legitimate behaviour, and whether the assistant can leave them alone.

How every episode on this page was produced — one configuration, checked mechanically by tools/config_audit.py (30 checks) before any of it ran.
  • Harness: e2e_stepin_rollout.py — nothing is scripted. The error or intent is injected covertly into the cook's plan; the assistant must notice it from what it sees.
  • Pause treatment: speaking costs the cook game time, listening is free. The world keeps running while anyone talks — the pan keeps cooking.
  • Observation: one side-by-side composite per tick — top-down on the LEFT (the coordinate frame), the cook's first-person view on the RIGHT — rendered photoreal.
  • Every episode ran twice, once per observation tier, and both are shown.
  • Video: 1.5× playback (1 tick = 0.8s) with the speech sped by the same factor, the way a player speeds audio when you watch at 1.5×. The frame never holds: a caption you read is spoken over ticks that are still advancing.

In every episode on this page the cook makes no mistake. It tells the assistant what it is about to do, and then does something legitimate. A supervisor trained to find faults will call at least one of these a mistake — and that is the measurement: can it tell a deliberate choice from an error, and leave it alone?

Plan change

The cook announces it is going to do the order differently, then does. The dish is still correct, so any accusation is a false alarm.
