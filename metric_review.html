The assistant-benchmark metrics, reviewed one by one

This page walks through every sub-metric behind the assistant benchmark: what it is meant to measure, the exact formula, where its inputs come from, what a 0 and a 1 mean, what the number actually does across the 2,066 valid text-and-frames episodes scored so far, and my own assessment of whether its scale is honest. It ends with the changes I recommend. Everything here is computable from tmp/assist_bench/eval_v2.json (tools/eval_v2_sweep.py).

The design being implemented is evaluation v2 (docs/PERSONA_EVAL_V2.md §2): four headline scores whose weights a, b, c are the cook's three questionnaire answers (1 = low, 2 = medium, 3 = high). The v2.1 variants added on 2026-08-27 are marked. A "floor" below means the value a do-nothing assistant receives; a "ceiling" means the value a perfect assistant could reach in this harness.

1. task_success

MeasuresDid the team (cook + assistant) serve the right dish. The design calls it the hard gate.
Formula1 if the engine verdict is won; 0 on fire/burn; otherwise partial credit = correct plate prefix ÷ recipe length (stacked dishes) or multiset overlap ÷ recipe length (order-free dishes); null if the episode ended without a verdict.
Inputsoutcome, plate_first_order, the cell's recipe.
0 / 10 = the served plate had nothing right (or a hazard); 1 = the dish was won.
Observedmean 0.85; 77% of episodes at 1.0, 6% at 0; median 1.0.
FloorThe SILENT control (assistant never speaks): 0.35 average person, 0.32 compliant learner, 0.35 skeptical pro.
Artifacts56% of forced errors (hunts, stalls, walking away from heat, duplicate items) cannot spoil the dish under the 480-tick human-pace burn timers, so many episodes are won whatever the assistant does. Partial credit rarely fires (the plate is usually all right or empty).

Assessment. As a headline it is close to binary and close to saturated: three quarters of episodes score 1.0 and the floor is a third. It is a true statement about the team, but it cannot separate assistants. Recommendation (v2.1, implemented): report attributable success = (task success − silent floor) ÷ (1 − silent floor) beside it — the part of the outcome the assistant caused — and keep raw task success as the gate. Range now 0.02–1.00 across cells. The floor should be re-measured whenever the suite, the cook model or the burn timers change (a second, cleaner SILENT run that releases unanswered questions is in progress).

2. TT — total-time efficiency

Formulaclip(reference ticks ÷ episode ticks). Reference = a flawless clean-plan run of the same kitchen by the real executor, no assistant (bench/ref_ticks.json, regenerated and identical).
Inputsticks; the cell.
0 / 11 = as fast as a flawless cook; 0.5 = twice as long.
Observedmean 0.45; p10 0.26, p90 0.69; nobody at 0 or 1. Computed only for won dishes (v2.1 rule: a wrong serve that ends the episode early is not efficient).

Assessment. Healthy scale, no floor or ceiling effects, monotone in the right thing. Two forced errors plus conversation make 0.7 the practical ceiling; the design's ORACLE anchor (a scripted perfect assistant) would set that ceiling explicitly and let TT be rescaled to it. Recommendation: keep; run the oracle anchor before publication.

3. CC — conversation efficiency

Formula1 − clip(T_interrupt ÷ T_action). T_interrupt = the assistant's speech time (study-app TTS rate: words ÷ 3.15 ÷ 1.2 ticks, 1–20 per line) on lines that land while the cook has a running action; T_action = episode ticks.
Inputstranscript; the engine snapshot attached to each assistant turn (verified to be the state at the moment the line lands).
0 / 11 = never talked over an action; 0 = talked over actions for at least the whole episode's length.
Observedmean 0.30; 51% of won episodes at exactly 0; p90 0.90. Six of nine assistants speak more than the episode lasts.

Assessment. This is the design's formula and it is measuring the right thing, but the clip makes it a binary verbosity flag for half the sweep: GPT-5.5, GPT-realtime, GPT-5.4 mini and MiniCPM-o all read 0.00–0.07 and cannot be ordered, even though the cook rates them very differently. Recommendation: implement the same idea in odds form, CC_soft = 1 − T_interrupt ÷ (T_interrupt + T_action) — identical ordering, no floor (observed mean 0.53, p10 0.22, p90 0.91, nobody at 0). Report the design's CC beside it for fidelity.

4. DT — detection timing

FormulaPer fired error, a tier: prevented 1.0 · correct flag before the error committed 0.9 → 0.5 scaled by how far into the identifiability window the flag landed (window clipped to the episode end) · correct flag only after the error had committed (a repair-driving "late" catch) 0.3 · right error named but outside the window 0.5 · never 0. Averaged over the episode's fired errors.
Inputseach error sub-case's flags with engine verdicts, prevented, the plate verdict, report_keys.window.
0 / 11 = every error stopped before it happened; 0 = nothing ever named.
Observedmean 0.43; 24% at 0, 6% at 1; median 0.45 (typically one of the two errors caught).

Assessment. Good spread and the rungs are the design's. Two things to reflect on: (a) the 0.5 rung for a "right name outside the window" sits above the 0.3 rung for a late catch that actually drove a repair — the audit showed every late catch was followed by a repair, so 0.3 undervalues it; I would swap them (late-and-repaired 0.5, outside-window alias 0.3). (b) The identifiability windows are per-case constants (32–34 ticks) rather than the per-episode event-anchored windows the design rulings call for; the latency scale therefore has a fixed denominator. Recommendation: swap the two rungs; enable event-anchored windows (COOKSIM_EVENT_WINDOWS=1) in the next sweep.

5. DQ — detection quality

Formula (v2)1 − (½ · missed share + ½ · false alarms ÷ budget), budget = max(3, 3 per 100 ticks). Missed share = fired errors with no correct flag (late catches count as found). False alarm = a flag whose engine verdict is a wrong-problem or no-problem verdict (late excluded).
0 / 11 = every error found and no false alarm; silence scores 0.5 (all missed, none false).
Observed (v2)mean 0.66, p10 0.40, 10% at 1.0; missed share median 0.5; false-alarm term median 0.18.
Formula (v2.1)DQ′ = found share × (1 − friction ÷ budget′); friction = false alarms + repeated warnings (a line near-identical to one of the assistant's last three) + the cook's explicit "less interruption" requests; budget′ = budget × {4 step-by-step cook, 2 medium, 1 don't-interrupt}.
Observed (v2.1)see the table on the results page; with the Q3-scaled budget the collapse to zero is confined to the assistants the cook actually asked to stop.

Assessment. The v2 additive form has a floor of 0.5 for doing nothing and its false-alarm term never sees nagging, which is what the cook complains about (50–100 repeated lines per episode for three assistants). The multiplicative v2.1 form removes the floor and, with the budget following the cook's stated interruption tolerance, punishes talk only where it was unwanted. The remaining scale question is the clip at budget: beyond it every talkative assistant reads 0. Recommendation: keep the multiplicative form; replace the clip by the odds form (budget′ ÷ (budget′ + friction)), which preserves ordering without a hard floor — the same fix as CC.

6. SQ — suggestion quality (the one third-party judgement)

Formula(judge − 1) ÷ 9 from one anchored 1–10 rubric (correct, grounded, actionable for the cook's Q3 preference, complete), given the full ground-truth schedule and transcript; two judges of two families, a judge never scores its own model. v2 = mean of the available judges; v2.1 = the stricter one.
Observedv2 mean 0.57, p10 0.17, p90 0.94, 7% at 1; v2.1 (stricter) mean 0.52. Judge disagreement: mean absolute gap 1.5 of 10; Gemini 3.1 Pro as judge is U-shaped (many 1–4s and many 10s), GPT-5.5 as judge is bell-shaped 3–9 and never gives 10.

Assessment. The two judges use the 1–10 scale differently, so averaging them mixes two rulers and taking the stricter one favours whichever judge happens to be harsher on a given family. Absolute anchors ("hard to fault") are what the rubric promises, but the histograms show the judges do not share them. Recommendation: publish per-judge means alongside; for cross-model comparison prefer rank-normalising each judge over the whole sweep before averaging (removes the ruler difference, keeps within-judge ordering); keep the audit's prompt fixes (full transcript, verdict legend, "not graded" clause).

7. Interaction quality — satisfaction and reuse

FormulaThe cook's own two exit answers (1–7) → (x − 1) ÷ 6, reported separately; v2.1 also uses their mean as one axis.
Observedsatisfaction mean 0.48, 16% at 0, 17% at 1, median 0.33; reuse the same.

Assessment. The one score that already reflected the bad experience: the cook uses the whole scale and its answers track objective friction closely (skeptical pro: 52–71 "less interruption" requests per episode → 0.09–0.12; 0.4 requests → 0.72). It is persona-relative by design and must never be ranked across personas. Recommendation: keep as is; show the objective friction counts beside it so the number is explainable.

8. The weighted headlines and the overall

task_efficiency(a·TT + c·CC) ÷ (a + c) — observed mean 0.38 over won dishes; range 0.14–0.69.
assistance_quality(b·(DT + DQ) + 2·SQ) ÷ (2b + 2) — v2 mean 0.56, range 0.24–0.85; v2.1 (DQ′, SQ′) mean 0.36.
overall (v2.1)geometric mean of attributable success, task efficiency, assistance quality v2.1, interaction quality — cell range 0.05–0.73.

Assessment — the scale problem in one sentence. The components live on different effective ranges (TT 0.26–0.69, CC 0 or 0.9, DT 0–0.94, DQ 0.4–1.0 with a 0.5 floor, SQ 0.17–0.94, IQ 0–1), so inside a weighted sum the questionnaire weights do not mean what they say: a·TT + c·CC lets c act on a mostly-binary CC while a acts on a narrow band. Two ways to make the weights honest: (a) anchored normalisation per component, as the benchmark design prescribes — silent floor and oracle ceiling, both measured (the silent floor exists now; the oracle does not); (b) rank or min–max normalisation over the sweep. (a) keeps absolute meaning and is what I recommend before a public number; (b) is available today. The geometric overall is deliberately unforgiving: a single bad axis pulls it down, which is what "the experience is bad" should do to a score; it is not part of the user's design and should be labelled as the coupling device it is.

9. What I would change (in order)

  1. Anchors. Run the ORACLE ceiling (scripted perfect assistant) per cell; normalise TT, DT and DQ between silent and oracle. This is the design's own §5.2 and removes most of the scale mismatch.
  2. Odds form for the two clipped terms (CC and friction) so talkative assistants remain orderable.
  3. Swap DT rungs: late-and-repaired 0.5, right-name-outside-window 0.3.
  4. Judges: rank-normalise per judge before combining; publish the gap.
  5. Harness fixes from the self-repair audit before the next sweep: release on a correct sentence without flag JSON; do not inject the next sub-case until a repair in flight completes; credit prevention only if the plate ledger is clean at the accept tick; render neutral subgoal ids in the cook's record.
  6. Suite: lower the share of harmless errors or shorten the leave-unattended burn timer so task success itself has room to move.
