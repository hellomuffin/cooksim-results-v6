How streaming / online video benchmarks design their questions — and what CookSim should borrow

Survey date 2026-08-27. Purpose: redesign the cook→assistant question probes in CookSim, which today are single-hop state look-ups ("is the fryer done?", "what's on the plate?", "did I already chop the tomato?", "where's the cutting board?"). The field has converged on a small set of question families that are demonstrably hard for current models; all of them are cheap to instantiate from a simulator's ground-truth trace.

0. The organising formula

Every streaming benchmark since 2024 fits one of two interaction contracts (the 2026 survey by Yang et al. [39] states them as the proactive and reactive paradigms):

$$\text{reactive: } P(y_t \mid V_{\le t}, Q_t) \qquad\qquad \text{proactive: } a_t \sim P(a_t \mid V_{\le t}),\; a_t=1 \Rightarrow y_t \sim P(y_t \mid V_{\le t})$$

A reactive question is asked at a timestamp $t$ and must be answered from the causal prefix $V_{\le t}$ only; a proactive instruction is given once and the model must decide when $a_t=1$. Questions are further indexed by where the evidence lies relative to $t$: past (backward tracing), present (real-time perception), or future (forward responding). This three-way split — introduced by OVO-Bench [5] and adopted by OVBench [32], RIVER [44] and StreamEQA — is the taxonomy CookSim should adopt. CookSim's present probes all sit in the present / single-hop cell; the difficult cells (past-vs-now, counting-over-time, hallucination, forward/proactive) are empty.

2. What makes questions hard — the consensus list

Across the benchmarks the cells with the largest human–model gap are consistent:

  1. Hallucination / never-happened. OVO-Bench HLD 52.6 vs 91.4 human [5]; OVBench's Temporal Hallucination Verification [32]; MAGQA's "Not Mentioned" segments [38]; OmniPro's pre-probe that expects "no" [36]. Asking about an event that has not (yet) occurred is the single cheapest hard question.
  2. Counting over time, especially deduplicated or cumulative (TOMATO Action Count 36–43 vs 94 [23]; TemporalBench "3 slices vs 2" [22]; MVBench Moving Count ≈ chance [21]; OmniPro Cumulative/Deduplicated Counting [36]).
  3. Order and sequence (VidEgoThink: "lack the ability to judge order or sequence" [25]; OVO ASI; OVBench Action Sequence; VStream "before/after" Y/N [9]).
  4. Past-vs-now state change and re-asked questions — the same question at two timestamps with different answers (StreamingBench's cards example at 00:33 and 01:10 [2]; OmniMMI Dynamic State Grounding, accuracy collapsing after the first state [26]; OVBench "8 seconds ago" [32]).
  5. Duration ("How long has …", "which step took the longest" [11, 32]).
  6. Multi-hop referring across turns (OmniMMI ##ANSWER## chains [27]; StreamingBench Sequential QA; SVBench QA chains [33]; StreamBench Conversational Interaction [31]).
  7. Distractors from the same video at another time (OVBench [32]) — the MCQ device that forces temporal grounding rather than recognition.
  8. Forward / anticipation (EgoPlan 38 % [18]; OVO FPD; OVBench Future Prediction).
  9. When-to-speak. Every proactive benchmark reports that timing is harder than content: "Deciding intervention timing is harder than content quality" (GuideMe [37]); ProReady-QA's asymmetric early/late penalties [40]; streaming models score ≈0 on StreamingBench Proactive Output [1, 28].
  10. Error detection vs instruction delivery. Models give next-step instructions (sF1 ≈ 42–50) far better than they catch mistakes (sF1 ≈ 34–42) [37]; fine-grained quantity/temperature mistakes are the weakest [42, 49].

3. Programmatic generation for CookSim

Trace model. Let the engine log $\mathcal{E}=\{(t, a, o, s)\}$ (tick, cook action, object, station), state transitions $\mathcal{S}=\{(t, o, k, v^-\!\to v^+)\}$ (attribute $k$ of object $o$ changes at $t$), plate contents $P(t)$, cook position $x(t)$, and assistant utterances $\mathcal{U}=\{(t, u)\}$. Write $\text{state}(o,k,t)$ for the value of attribute $k$ at tick $t$, $\text{last}(\cdot)$/$\text{first}(\cdot)$ for extremal ticks, and $t_q$ for the ask tick. Because the engine is deterministic, forward quantities (ready/burn ticks) and counterfactual rollouts are also queryable. Following OVBench, MCQ distractors should be true answers at other ticks of the same episode; following OmniPro, every proactive template should be evaluated both in probe mode (query at $t_s-\Delta$ expects "not yet") and in online mode (utterance tick $t_a$ graded by the ARS formula in §1.3).

Practical rules from the survey. (i) Generate each template at 3–5 ask ticks per episode so the same question has different gold answers (StreamingBench's five timestamps). (ii) Mix in a fixed share of never-happened instantiations (templates 3, 7, 13-pre-probe) so that "no / not yet" is a frequent correct answer. (iii) Build MCQ distractors from the same episode at other ticks, never from other episodes. (iv) Grade proactive templates by utterance tick with the ARS early/late asymmetry and report Correct-Silent / False-Alarm / No-Response counts, not only accuracy. (v) Keep the human silent by default (ProAssist's no_talk / talk_some profiles) so that question density itself is a controlled variable.
