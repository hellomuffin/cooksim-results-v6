The cook is a text agent on every tier. It never sees a frame; it reads a state map that names every object with an id and an (x,y) coordinate, and it commands its body exactly — go to fryer 2. The assistant is a vision agent. It watches side-by-side rendered frames plus an exact-state readout, and it is told to talk like a person in a kitchen. The cook, for its part, is forbidden from ever saying an id or a coordinate out loud. So the two agents hold precise knowledge in incompatible currencies, and the conversation between them is ordinary spatial English anchored to the top-down view they share — the fryer in the bottom-right corner, the board nearest the serving pass. Only one of them can cash that in. Translating the assistant's description into the exact object is the cook's own work, and it is the work this benchmark measures.

Every prompt, map, readout and refusal below was produced by importing the engine and calling it, on the episode stamped above. Nothing here is transcribed.

The contract, in four lines

The cook may name an object by id or by coordinate; a bare name works when the kind has one instance.

Content and state selectors — the empty fryer, the pan with the steak — work when they pick out exactly one.

When a kind has more than one instance, the engine refuses a bare name and asks which — always, even when the instances are empty and indistinguishable. It never guesses.

The cook never speaks an id or a coordinate. The assistant is asked to talk like a person, but is no longer told the same in so many words.

== THE KITCHEN AND ITS RULES (how this kitchen works — the same physics the assistant knows) ==
Ingredients come from their SOURCE crates ("pick up" with empty hands takes one raw item).
A RAW item needs nothing: it goes onto the plate straight from its crate ("scoop" while
holding the plate).
A CHOPPED item: carry the raw item to a cutting board, "put down", then "chop" (several
presses) until it is chopped; a chopped item is scooped from the board onto your held plate,
or picked up to be cooked if the recipe wants it cooked.
A COOKED item: put the raw (or, for some foods, chopped) item into a heat station that
accepts it, "turn on" the station with empty hands, and wait for its PROGRESS BAR to fill;
then scoop it out onto your held plate. Cooking stations accept exactly these inputs and refuse everything else — the pot takes ONLY: chopped mushroom, noodles, chopped onion, chopped potato, rice, chopped tomato; the pan takes ONLY: egg, fish, meat, shrimp, chopped steak; the fryer takes ONLY: fish, chopped potato, shrimp; the oven takes ONLY: dough.
Plating = HOLD a clean plate ("pick up" at the plate stack) and "scoop" each ready item
onto it. An ORDERED recipe must be plated in exactly the listed order. A plate can rest only
on an EMPTY COUNTER ("put down") — never on a board, a station or a crate; to pick up a raw
item your hands must be empty. Anything on a plate cannot be taken off again: a wrong item
means "trash" the plate's contents at the trash tile and rebuild it.
Serving ("serve" at the pass while holding the plate) with a wrong or incomplete plate is
REJECTED and ends the episode.
Food that has just finished cooking sits safely on the heat for 25 ticks; after that the
station SMOKES, and after 60 more ticks it catches FIRE; 15 ticks of fire and the
kitchen is lost. Smoke stops only when you scoop the food out. Time passes while you talk.
