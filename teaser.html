The controlled comparison

Assistants 1 and 4 face the same user type and differ only in when they speak; assistants 2 and 3 send the identical warning and differ only in who is cooking. That is the whole argument of the paper in one picture: the assistant's sentence is not the outcome — the rollout is.

What the metrics show

  • dish accepted separates assistant 4 from the rest — the one thing a transcript cannot fake.
  • warning came separates prevention (assistant 1) from a late catch (2 and 3) from a miss (4); in the paper this is the detection-timing term.
  • steps to finish is the efficiency term: all three wins are wins, but the user's disposition costs 32 and 49 extra steps.
  • turns of talk is the conversation cost the persona-weighted efficiency term charges for.
  • advice quality is the judged rubric score, which drops for the step-by-step hand-holding and collapses for the assistant that never warns.
