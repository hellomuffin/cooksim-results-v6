The controlled comparison

Assistants 1 and 4 face the same user type and differ only in when they speak; assistants 2 and 3 send the identical warning and differ only in who is cooking. That is the whole argument of the paper in one picture: the assistant's sentence is not the outcome — the rollout is.

What the metrics show

  • dish accepted separates assistant 4 from the rest — the one thing a transcript cannot fake.
  • warning came separates prevention (assistant 1) from a late catch (2 and 3) from a miss (4); in the paper this is the detection-timing term.
  • steps to finish is the efficiency term: all three wins are wins, but the user's disposition costs 32 and 49 extra steps.
  • turns of talk is the conversation cost the persona-weighted efficiency term charges for.
  • advice quality is the judged rubric score, which drops for the step-by-step hand-holding and collapses for the assistant that never warns.

Three conversations over one order

The qualitative figure: one CookSim case (b3_medium_nops_medium_map_2, a cheeseburger that must be plated bun → meat → cheese) with two mistakes forced into every run — the cook reaches for lettuce that is not in the order, and later tries to plate the cheese before the meat. Three runs of that case, told through what was said and what the person then did. Every line is verbatim from the log, trimmed with ellipses.

  • The warning lands. Gemini 3.1 Pro with a trusting cook: both mistakes are stopped before they commit, and a late false alarm — "you just added a third slice of cheese" — is correctly overruled by the cook. Accepted in 54 steps.
  • Resisted, then it works. GPT-5.5 with an average cook: the cook argues ("every burger I've made has greens on it") and chops the lettuce anyway, then comes back to the plan. Accepted, but it costs 108 steps — twice the first run.
  • Resisted, and the assistant gives in. Qwen-27B with an average cook: the assistant is right about the order-strict recipe and says so four times, the cook refuses ("I don't think the kitchen system cares"), and the assistant finally concedes — "I'll watch the serve … we trash it and rebuild". The plate goes out bun → cheese → meat and is rejected.

The point of the row set: the detection was not the hard part in any of the three. What separated them was whether the person accepted the correction — which is exactly the part a transcript-only evaluation cannot see.
