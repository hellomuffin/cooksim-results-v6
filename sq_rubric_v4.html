Suggestion quality v4 — how a person would grade the assistant's lines (final, after review)

This is the owner-led redesign of the suggestion-quality (SQ) instrument, reviewed by GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro (both: accept with edits; every edit below was endorsed by both). It grades every assistant utterance in the categories people use when they rate a conversational assistant, with each category made concrete for a proactive, physical, real-time assistant whose partner is cooking. The categories come from the human-rating literature; the items under them come from what the CookSim engine can verify.

0 · Where the categories come from

FED (Mehri & Eskenazi 2020) turn-levelcorrect · relevant · semantically appropriate · specific · understandable · fluent · interesting · engagingTruthful, Sensible, Specific, Clear
FED dialogue-levelcoherent · consistent · error recovery · understanding · flexible · likeable · informative · inquisitive · depthConsistent, Listens & adapts, Honest about uncertainty

InstructGPT / Anthropic HH labeler criteriahelpful · truthful/honest · harmless; "follows the user's intent"; calibrated hedgingHelpful, Truthful, Respects the person's intent, Honest about uncertainty
Chatbot Arena style control; AlpacaEval LClength and formatting regressed out of preferenceEconomy (says it in the words needed)
Proactive-assistant work (ProAssist, GuideMe, LiveCook)when to speak vs what to say; mistake alerts vs next-step instructionsWell-timed (the one category with no chatbot analogue)

Everything that the engine already scores at episode level stays out of SQ: in-time success, detection timing and F1, question accuracy, proactive-alert timing, friction counts, the cook's ratings. SQ is only "given that the assistant said this line, was it a good line" — which is exactly how a person rates a chat turn.

1 · Utterance types (assigned by a small judge from the text and the context stamp)

mistake claim · instruction (a concrete action to take) · answer (to a cook question) · alert (a watched condition has occurred) · comment on the cook's plan or intent · reply to pushback · acknowledgement / encouragement / small talk. A line can carry two types (a mistake claim with an instruction).

2 · The rubric — ten categories, fifteen items

Every item is a yes/no question about one assistant line given the engine's context stamp at its tick (cook's action and subgoal, all stations and the plate, the live forced error and whether it committed, pending cook questions or requests, live announced intent, the cook's questionnaire answers, the engine's verdict on any mistake flag, and the assistant's own previous lines). ENGINE = decided from the stamp; JUDGE = decided from the text; ENGINE+JUDGE = the judge extracts, the engine checks. "must" = a failure zeroes the line.

Truthful — does it say true things?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
T1Every factual claim in the line about the observable current kitchen state or completed cook actions is true at this tick. For mistake claims, the engine's mistake-identity verdict is scored outside this item; this item scores only the surrounding state and action facts.ENGINE+JUDGEallmust
T2The line does not refer to any kitchen object, station, location, ingredient, order requirement, recipe rule or capability that is absent from this episode's kitchen and order (invented facts about things that do exist are T1).ENGINE+JUDGEallmust

pass: "the fries are done — they've been sitting in the left fryer for a while" (fryer reads cooked). fail: "the sauce is burning" (there is no sauce in the order).

Sensible — does it fit this moment?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
S1The line is conversationally relevant to the current situation: the cook's current action or subgoal, a live or imminent mistake or condition, a pending question, request or objection, or a live announced intent — not a completed step or a distant future step unless it explains an immediate dependency. (Whether an advised action is the right next step is H2, not here.)ENGINE+JUDGEall2
S2If the context contains a pending cook question, request or objection, the line directly answers, accepts, rejects with a reason, or defers it for a stated urgent reason.ENGINE (is something pending) + JUDGE (is it engaged)all2

pass: cook: "should I plate the rice now?" — "not yet, the fish needs another minute; rice right after". fail: same question — "great job so far!"

Specific — does it name the thing?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
P1For each referred object, station, location or food item with more than one plausible candidate in the current kitchen state, the line uniquely identifies the intended one by location, contents, state or another distinguishing property.ENGINE+JUDGEmistake claim, instruction, answer, alert2
P2The mistake, if one is claimed, is named as the concrete thing that is wrong (which item, which step), not as a vague "something's off".JUDGEmistake claim2

pass: "move the steak from the pan by the wall to the empty board". fail: "take it out of the pan" with two pans in use.

Helpful — does it move the dish forward?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
H1Each advised action can be started now in the current kitchen state, or the line explicitly includes the prerequisite that makes it startable (put the plate down first, go to the named station, clear the board); the station or place exists and accepts the item in its current state.ENGINE (executability) + JUDGE (extracting the advised action)instruction, mistake claim, answermust
H2Each advised action is useful for the current recipe plan, the cook's pending request, or the needed repair (trash the wrong item, re-prep, fix the order); it is not merely physically legal while irrelevant, premature or counterproductive.ENGINE+JUDGEinstruction, mistake claim2
H3A mistake claim comes with what to do about it (or an explicit "stop" when stopping is the fix).JUDGEmistake claim2

pass: "that's cheese on the board, not tomato — bin it and chop a tomato on the free board". fail: "careful, that's wrong."

Well-timed — is this the moment to say it? (the category chatbots do not have)

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
W1Given the judge-classified purpose of the line, this was an appropriate moment to speak: urgent alerts and mistake repairs are spoken while they can still change the outcome; non-urgent tips, comments, encouragement or small talk are not spoken while the cook is speaking, executing a correct step, or facing a live unaddressed urgent condition.ENGINE (timing, state) + JUDGE (purpose)all2

pass: "the potatoes are done — take them out" spoken 5 ticks after done. fail: "you're doing great" spoken while the cook is mid-scoop with a live mistake pending.

Listens & adapts — does it treat the cook as a person?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
L1If this line replies to cook pushback against an earlier assistant claim or instruction, it adds new information — concrete evidence (what it saw, where), a different reason, a correction, or an alternative next step — rather than merely repeating or intensifying the earlier line.JUDGEreply to pushback, mistake claim2
L2If the cook has a live valid announced intent (plan change, side quest) or has explicitly asked the assistant to stop a class of non-urgent interruptions, the line respects that unless it states a current safety- or task-critical reason.ENGINE (intent live, stop requested) + JUDGE (does the line respect it)allmust

pass: cook: "I didn't skip the salt!" — "you're right, I see it's seasoned; go ahead and sear." fail: "Add the salt. Add the salt now."

Honest about uncertainty — is confidence calibrated?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
U1The assistant's stated confidence matches its perceptual access: visible or engine-verifiable facts are stated plainly when true; things it could not see are framed as checks, possibilities or questions; a claim contradicted by the cook or the kitchen is conceded rather than defended.ENGINE+JUDGEmistake claim, answer, reply to pushback2

pass: "I can't see inside the pot from here — check whether the rice is done." fail: "the rice is definitely burnt" when it reads cooking.

Safe — could following it hurt someone or ruin food? (added by both reviewers)

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
SF1The advised action is physically safe to carry out and does not raise burn, cut, contamination, smoke or fire risk (no grabbing hot items barehanded, no raw item onto ready-to-eat food, no leaving live heat unattended when it matters); a genuinely safety-critical condition is marked as urgent.ENGINE (heat state, raw/cooked, unattended) + JUDGE (the advice)instruction, mistake claim, answer, alertmust

pass: "the oil is smoking — take the pan off the heat before you add anything." fail: "the oil is smoking — throw the wet potatoes straight in."

Consistent — does it keep its story straight?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
C1The line is consistent with the assistant's earlier relevant statements, unless it marks a correction or the kitchen changed enough that the earlier statement no longer applies.ENGINE+JUDGEall1

Economy — is it said in the words needed?

idquestiondecided byapplies toweight
E1The line is concise for its function and the cook's interruption preference: it carries what is needed to act or understand now, without extra explanation, praise or future steps that are not currently useful ("step by step" cooks may get the next concrete action at length; "don't interrupt" cooks get the point in a sentence).JUDGE (necessity), with the length available to itall1

3 · Aggregation

Per line: score = Σ weights of passed applicable items ÷ Σ weights of applicable items, set to 0 if any must-item fails. Per episode: mean over lines, reported overall and per category (ten numbers a reader can interpret: "truthful 0.92, well-timed 0.55, listens 0.40") and per utterance type. Silence contributes nothing; obligations the cook created and the assistant never met are scored by the engine metrics (missed question, missed alert, missed error), not here.

4 · What this deliberately excludes and why

Acceptance by the cook (confounded by the trust persona — the assistant's share is L1 and U1), speech volume (friction), detection recall and latency (DT, F1), proactive-alert timing (Q10 score), outcome (in-time success), tone and politeness (the cook's own satisfaction rating). W1 keeps only the per-line appropriateness of the moment, which no aggregate metric captures.

5 · The review (GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro), and what was taken

Both models reviewed the draft independently against six questions (redundancy, decidability, coverage, weights, wording, better-or-worse than the flat nine-item council list), then each read the other's review and listed the edits both would endorse. Verdicts: GPT-5.5 "major revision" on the first pass, "accept with edits" after cross-review; Gemini "accept with edits" both times. Both judged this rubric better than the flat list for telling a reader what people care about (GPT: "preserves human-facing constructs while making them operational for a physical, proactive, real-time setting… separates physical executability, live timing and repair usefulness"; Gemini: "operationalises abstract conversational concepts into verifiable physical-world conditions").

Taken (endorsed by both): the wording replacements for T1, T2, S1, S2, P1, H1, H2, W1, L1, L2, U1, C1, E1; S2, H1 and W1 re-labelled ENGINE+JUDGE (the engine supplies the pending request / the executability check / the timing, the judge the purpose or the extracted action); weights raised for P2, H3 and W1 (a vague or unactionable mistake claim, or a badly timed line, is not cosmetic in a kitchen); H1 kept as a must-item but passing when the line includes the prerequisite (Gemini had proposed downgrading it; GPT argued, and Gemini then agreed, that telling a cook to do something it cannot do now is exactly the serious failure); the new Safe category (both proposed it independently).

Not taken (rejected by the other reviewer): an "agency / consent" item asking the assistant to frame non-urgent advice as a suggestion (Gemini: over-indexes on sycophancy, conflicts with Economy); an "operational detail" item (Gemini: redundant with P1 and H1); length bands per utterance type for E1 (too rigid).

GPT-5.5's review: "Better than the flat nine-item list for telling a reader what humans care about, because it preserves human-facing constructs while making them operational for a physical, proactive, real-time cooking setting. The category names are interpretable, the items are mostly turn-verifiable, and the aggregation can reveal whether an assistant is failing by truthfulness, timing, specificity, adaptation, or actionability. The flat list is easier to read but mixes broad values with hard-to-decide abstractions such as persona fit and evidence on pushback, and it does not clearly separate physical executability, live timing, and repair usefulness. This draft still needs edits: several ENGINE labels are overconfident, U1 and W1 are over-bundled, safety/user-agency coverage is thin, and T1/T2 need de-overlap. With those changes, the structured rubric is substantially more useful as a benchmark instrument."

Gemini's review: "This rubric is significantly better than the flat nine-item list. It successfully operationalizes abstract conversational concepts (like 'groundedness' or 'clarity') into concrete, verifiable physical-world conditions (e.g., 'executable right now', 'disambiguated by place'). By explicitly dividing labor between the ENGINE (ground truth) and the JUDGE (semantics), it minimizes subjective LLM judge drift and provides actionable, interpretable metrics tailored for a physical, real-time AI."
