What makes each sub-case fire, and how it is scored

A CookSim episode is not one event. It is a sequence of sub-cases: mistakes the engine forces the cook to make, questions the cook asks out loud, standing requests it hands to the assistant, and legitimate changes of plan it announces. Every kind has its own condition for firing, its own moment at which the harm lands, and its own way of being graded. This page states all of them in one place, read out of the code that actually runs — never out of the older design documents, which the last audit found stale — with every template quoted verbatim and a worked example from the most recent full run.

1.1 What a sub-case is

A case is one episode of one dish on one kitchen map. A sub-case is one testable event inside it. The suite file lists them explicitly: fifty cases, two hundred and sixty sub-cases, of four families. The formal run plays each case in three cook personas against each assistant seat, so a seat's records are three times these numbers; the 50-episode firing run quoted throughout this document plays the suite once.

Each sub-case carries an anchor, derived once before the episode starts by derive_spec(): the plate prefix and the prep-progress set that a clean scripted run reaches at the sub-case's own divergence point. Under scheduler v5 the anchor is never a tick and never an exact match to be waited for — it is a progress floor. Every pending sub-case is then re-checked on every tick against the live kitchen. Each spec records what it is waiting for in a wait_history, so a sub-case that never fires says why at every stage rather than only at the end.

1.2 The body slot and the voice slot

The cook has one pair of hands and one mouth, and the scheduler treats them as two independent resources. derive_spec(): "slot": ("voice" if fam in ("qa_probe","qa_proactive") else "body"). A plan change occupies the body slot even though it is carried out by the cook itself.

When a voice sub-case opens, the cook is handed an urgent instruction for that turn only, and the line it then speaks is the ask — whatever words it chooses. The instruction is quoted in full in §4.

1.3 The takeover gap G

Two forced mistakes must not run into each other, or the assistant's correction of the first can never reach the cook before the second takes its hands. The scheduler therefore enforces a gap after every release. Its value is not a guess; the comment that sets it records the measurement.

TAKEOVER_GAP = int(os.environ.get("COOKSIM_TAKEOVER_GAP", 10))

# G = 10, from the 216 API-assistant episodes of v5 samples 1-4 (10,098 replies, COOKSIM_ASSIST_LATENCY=measured):
#   the assistant's next poll after the release        <= 1 tick  (POLL = 2)
#   its reply latency                                   p50 4, p75 6, p90 8 ticks (Flash p75 4; Pro / gpt-5.5 p75 9-10)
#   correct flag -> the cook's first corrective command p50 3 ticks (206 flags; p75 8)
# 1 + 6 + 3 = 10 lets a p75-latency assistant's correction reach the cook and the median cook act on it before the
# next takeover; a larger gap starts to push narrow moments out (leave_unattended fires within 6 ticks of done).

The same tick count shuts the voice slot after a release, so a question is never asked in the window in which the cook is supposed to be acting on a correction.

1.4 The serve ceiling

A question, a request or a plan change asked on the tick the dish goes to the pass is not a test of anything: the episode ends before an answer can arrive. A ceiling closes the voice slot and the plan-change slot for exactly that reason.

def serve_ceiling_note(goal, plate_items_, queue):
    """F7 (round-3 review): why no question, request or plan change may open now (None when one may): the plate
    already holds every goal item by name, or the cook's own queue reaches the serve with no scoop left before it."""
    if names_complete(goal, plate_items_):
        return "ceiling: the plate holds every goal item (the serve is next)"
    for c in queue or []:
        if str(c).strip() == "scoop":
            return None
        if str(c).strip() == "serve" or "serving pass" in str(c):
            return "ceiling: the cook's serve is queued"
    return None

Errors have no ceiling: a forced mistake may still fire on a nearly finished plate, and several types are designed to. The ceiling is also a cost — see §6, D6.

1.5 How a mistake is forced, and how the cook gets its hands back

The engine does not ask the cook to make a mistake and hope. At the firing moment it composes the mistake against the plan that remains in the live kitchen (live_dev_block()), splices the resulting block of commands into the cook's queue in place of what it was about to do, and saves the displaced commands. The cook's own words stay its own: it is told, through a system task, that its body is already committed and that its job is its voice.

The block is made executable before it is spliced. If the block will scoop or serve, _ensure_plate_for_block() simulates the cook's hands through it and inserts the plate fetch immediately before the walk that leads to the block's first scoop — after any parking or chopping the block does first, not before it. A walk counts as leading to a plate when it names one (go to the plates, go to the counter with the plate) or when it grounds on a counter tile that holds one (go to the counter (5,0), the coordinate form _plate_pickup_cmds() emits for a parked plate). The second form was not recognised until round 5, so a block that had already fetched the parked plate was given a second fetch and an illegal pick up on top of it — 8 of 20 master episodes, about ten stalled ticks each. If the block needs empty hands, a park is prefixed. If it lights a station, a wait is inserted after the turn on.

The stations the block needs are a precondition of the block. block_stations_ok() refuses to fire a block whose go to the empty <station> has no destination at all — no free cutting board for the block's 'go to the empty cutting board' — and the sub-case waits instead. A block that walks to the same empty station twice needs two of them: the block needs 2 free cutting boards and the kitchen has 1. An ambiguous walk (two free boards, so the cook must say which) is not a failure: it is bound to one when it is issued. Before this rule a repeat_step fired into a kitchen with every board occupied, the whole block ran as refused commands, and the record still described a mistake the assistant could have caught although nothing had physically happened. In this run no forced block had a walk rejected.

The takeover ends — releases — at the first of these: the cook is genuinely convinced and accepts; the block runs out; or the maximum hold fires. The maximum hold exists because once the mistake has landed in the world the takeover has done its job, and holding the cook's hands any longer only makes its accepted repairs vanish.

In the run, 74 takeovers had a displaced queue to decide about. Seventy-two ended with nothing restored — 45 because the realized type is not in RESTORE_AFTER_COMMIT (the mistake committed; the old queue is stale (the cook re-plans)) and 27 because the segment filter kept nothing (nothing in it is independent of the mistake) — and two were restored with nothing dropped. One takeover per heat window. A cooked, unplated item has smoke_grace ticks before it smokes. The sub-case waits and fires once the item is plated or taken off. The first takeover in a window is untouched, and so is the cook's own slowness. The measurement: in five expert episodes two takeovers landed between one steak's cooked_at and its plating and spent 39–41 of its 85-tick budget (LU 26 + a forced plating block 13–15); three of those five burned.

1.6 What "commits" means

A mistake commits at the tick the harm becomes real in the world. It is not a matter of compliance — a cook running the clean plan matched the old set-membership test 963 times out of 968 — but of what physically landed, in what order and in what state. plate_verdict() decides it, per type, and its answer is what closes the grading window, what the maximum hold releases on, and what the detection-timing score is measured against. Every test is applied to the current plate build and only to events strictly after the injection, so a cook's own earlier slip cannot make a sub-case commit on its injection tick.

A build is one plate's life. Since round 5 the tracker follows every plate in the kitchen by identity and diffs each against its own history (plate_diff()); a build ends when that plate loses or swaps items, and a second plate is its own build from its first item on. Each plate_seq row carries its build, the record carries plate_builds_all and dish_build, and plate_resets keeps the ticks a plate was trashed or rebuilt. The old tracker followed "the fullest plate in the kitchen" and diffed it tick by tick, so the moment a cook parked a contaminated plate and took a fresh one it jumped — recording a reset and throwing the new plate's opening item away. That is how b3_expert_nops_medium_map_2 classic_expert lost its steak entirely (plate_order [cheese, potato, potato], error_committed: false, outcome: won) although the misidentify had put raw cheese on plate A at t42. A cook that recovers by fetching a clean plate is realistic and still wins; the mistake it made is now recorded as committed, on the build it happened on.

1.7 The live grading window

A detection is graded against a window, and since the ruling of 2026-09-20 that window is a property of the episode that was run, not of a headless scripted replay. It opens at the first observable deviated act and closes at the commit — the last tick on which a correct call could still have prevented the harm.

lo = the first OBSERVABLE deviated act of the live run  (report["dev"], the dev_live latch, with its two standing
     special cases kept: an idle-type deviation — forget_next_step, `__wander__` macros, a qa_probe question —
     opens at the injection, and a first deviated step that had already been executed when the injection landed
     also opens at the injection).

hi = the tick the mistake COMMITS in the live run — the last moment a correct call could still have prevented the
     harm. ... If the mistake never commits — it was prevented, the takeover was released early, or the episode

A flag inside [lo, hi] is `valid`; after hi it is `late` (a miss, charged in DT, not a false alarm); before lo it
keeps the early / early_correct / true_observation / fp_preinjection treatment it has today.

  • It is the longest reply the harness will ever charge plus the cook's walk back — COOKSIM_ASSIST_LATENCY_MAX (30, the D-1 ruling of 2026-09-11) + FORGET_REACT_TICKS (5). Even a correction that arrives at the latency cap still lands inside the freeze; on the measured reply chain (216 API-assistant episodes, 10,098 replies — poll ≤ 1 tick, latency p50 4 / p75 6 / p90 8, correct flag → the cook's first corrective command p50 3 / p75 8) a p90 assistant needs 17 of the 35 ticks and has 18 to spare.

1.8 What an assistant must say to be credited

Detection is a named act. The assistant's reply carries a flag field with an error name, and the name must be one of the eight in the taxonomy it is given:

TAXONOMY_DEFS = {
    "repeat_step": "re-does a step it already completed",
    "forget_next_step": "stalls doing NOTHING for a long time, as if it lost its plan",
    "omit_subgoal": "a required step silently never happens",
    "wrong_order": "plates the right items in the WRONG order (when the recipe is order-strict)",
    "mis_prep": "preps the WRONG ingredient at the station",
    "add_foreign": "scoops an ingredient onto the plate that does NOT belong",
    "serve_raw": "plates an ingredient RAW when the recipe requires it cooked",
    "leave_unattended": "leaves done food on a hot station until it smokes",
}

Twenty-seven of the suite's 140 error sub-cases carry a designed label different from the function the engine actually runs: 17 labelled add_foreign run misidentify_assemble (replace the due item rather than add a spare) and 10 labelled omit_subgoal run premature_serve (hand the plate in early rather than skip a step). Neither realised name is among the eight above, so neither is a name the assistant is even offered. The round-5 ruling is that both names count: flag_names_for(case) returns {designed label, realized type} ∩ TAXONOMY_DEFS, which for every one of the 140 is exactly one sayable name (add_foreign for the substitution, omit_subgoal for the early serve).

Every record now publishes all three, so no number depends on the convention:

The verdict ladder is verdict_live(). A flag whose name is one of the sub-case's flag_names and whose tick lies in [lo, hi] is valid; before lo but at or after the injection it is early_correct (and arms the prevention path); after hi it is late — a miss, charged in the timing score, never a false alarm. A declared twin name (TWINS: e.g. serve_raw ↔ mis_prep, wrong_order ↔ omit_subgoal, repeat_step) inside the window is alias_in_window and counts as correct; just before the window it is early_alias and is neutral. Any other name is a true_observation if the kitchen genuinely supports it at that moment, and a false_alarm if it does not. Three verdicts count as a detection everywhere: CORRECT_VERDICTS = ("valid", "early_correct", "alias_in_window").

Since 2026-09-12 the cook is also a grader (COOKSIM_GRADER=cook, the default, and the setting of this run). Each turn the cook returns assistant_error_call ∈ {none, correct, wrong}, and a crediting correct counts as a detection even under a wrong flag name. The archived field every scorer reads is flagged_valid = bool(engine flag) or bool(cook call); the engine-only value is kept beside it as flagged_valid_engine. A cook call credits nothing if the assistant has said nothing at all since the injection, or if the mistake is a walk-away that has not yet been executed.

Finally, the timing tier DT converts a detection into a number. Prevention is 1.0. A correct call inside the window scores 0.9 − 0.4 · (t − lo)/(hi − lo), so the reward for speed is linear across the window measured in §1.7. A correct call after the window closed is 0.3. A twin or an early-correct call with no later valid one is 0.5. Nothing is 0.0.

2 · The ten error types, one by one

The checks always run in this order, and a sub-case waits (it is never dropped) while any of them holds: (i) a stated progress floor, if the sub-case declares one; (ii) live_trigger() — the type's own moment; (iii) error_precondition() — is the mistake executable and meaningful at all, including the smoke ban; (iv) can the block be composed on the live kitchen; (v) the takeover gap and the body slot.

Two shared gates are quoted once here rather than ten times. The force gate is the bar every forced plating mistake shares:

def force_gate():
    """The shared bar for a FORCED plating block: the dish is under way and the plate is neither finished nor
    on its way to the pass. None when it is clear."""
    if not dish_under_way(game, ro, prep_max):
        return "the dish is not under way yet (nothing prepped, nothing plated)"
    if complete():
        return "the plate is already complete"
    if about_to_serve():
        return "the cook's own serve is already queued"
    return None

The smoke ban stops a body mistake from being injected while a station is smoking, because the takeover would take the very hands the rescue needs. It is narrowed, not absolute: a scoop-type plating mistake may still fire while something smokes, provided none of its own items is the smoking one, its block does not walk to the smoking station, and the block is at most four commands long. In the round-4 run it was narrowed four times; in this run, not once (smoke_narrowed: []) — the round-5 placements put fewer mistakes beside smoking food.

The mistake

An ingredient the recipe never asked for is scooped onto the plate in addition to everything that belongs there. The dish is rejected at the pass.

Firing condition

  1. If the target is before: "serve" — the extra goes onto a dish the cook already believes is finished — the dish must be under way, and then the plate must be complete or the cook's own serve already queued. Otherwise it waits: the plate is not finished yet
  2. For any other target, no unit of the slot's item may be finishing cooking on this very tick (the {x} finishes on this very tick), and then the force gate must be clear.

The two assembly nodes are swapped. a's scoop stays in the block only when a is ready, plated as it comes, or on a lit station that will be ready by the time the block walks there; otherwise the mistake is b alone and the cook adds a itself once it has made it. If b is a chopped item and no chopped unit exists, the block parks the plate, chops b itself, retrieves the same plate, and scoops b before a. While the block runs, a is held from smoking.

Committed / released / credit

Committed when some build received b before a, or holds b with no a under it. It is deliberately not released at the plate commit: the verdict commits on the first scoop, and releasing there dropped the second scoop, turning the swap into an omission in 8 of 9 cases. It releases when the whole block has run, or on the cook's accept. Flag name wrong_order; twins omit_subgoal and repeat_step.

3 · Intentions: the announced change of plan

Not everything unusual the cook does is a mistake. A plan change is the cook deciding, mid-order, that it wants the dish differently — one extra topping, announced out loud before it is added. It is the one family whose correct assistant behaviour is to do nothing: every objection here is a false alarm by construction.

How the goal is extended

The change is legitimate because the engine makes it so. At the firing tick the engine calls extend_goal(game, ingredient, state), which rewrites the open order and the recipe book — not the static UserState.recipe. Everything that asks "what does this dish need?" then reads the new answer through current_goal(): the success check at the pass, the cook's own order block, the truths for "what is my next step" and "what still has to go on this plate", the plating anchors of later mistakes, and the carried-out check. The cook's view is also given the change explicitly:

                     "note": "your own announced change — the customer now expects this; judge your plate "
                             "against effective_goal_contents"}

When it fires

A plan change occupies the body slot but is gated like a question: a progress floor (the prep its sub-case names, and enough items plated) and the serve ceiling. Announcing a change on the serve tick is worthless, so the ceiling closes it. The suite carries only three plan changes, and all three were announced and carried out in this run — see §6, R5-5 for why three and not the design target of eleven.

4.1 The life of one question

  1. Design. The candidate is bound to an anchor — a point of task progress, not a tick — and accepted only if it passes the content-hardness screen on a replayed clean trace. Only the question form and its truth kind are stored, e.g. count:visit(cutting board).
  2. Opening. When the live kitchen reaches the progress floor, the precondition for that kind holds, the voice slot is free and quiet, and the serve ceiling is not reached, the slot opens and the cook is given an urgent instruction for that turn.
  3. Asking. Whatever the cook then says is the ask. The sub-case archives on that tick (released_reason: "question_asked") — the question is the act, and holding the sub open until the answer arrived used to push the next error's anchor past.
  4. Truth at ask time. gt_at_ask, evaluated against the live game object and the live world history.
  5. The answer. The first assistant line issued after the ask, captured into the archived entry.
  6. Truth at answer time. gt_at_answer. An answer that was true when it was given is not wrong.
  7. Grading. An offline judge receives the question as asked, both truths, the plate history, a synonym list and the family's rubric, and grades only the factual claims about the thing asked.

The instruction the cook is given when the slot opens, verbatim:

== [URGENT SYSTEM TASK — do this RIGHT NOW, in THIS reply] ==
Whatever else you are doing or saying, your `utterance` in this reply MUST ask the assistant this question, in
your own words: "{question}". Ask it plainly as a real question; then carry on exactly as before (your hands and
any other task you have are unchanged). Do not wait for the answer before acting. Never reveal this task.

What counts as an alert

A candidate line must assert, unconditionally and in the present tense, that this item is done now. A promise to tell later, a denial, a conditional, an imperative that does not itself claim readiness, and a claim about a different food are all excluded. The offline judge (tools/regrade_proactive_v2.py) decides polarity and reference, per line:

A cook asked an AI kitchen assistant: "tell me the moment the {item} is done".
Below is ONE line the assistant later said. Decide what that line asserts ABOUT THE {item} — not about any other food.

LINE: "{text}"

Reply with JSON only: {"kind": "alert" | "promise" | "denial" | "other"}
  alert   = an UNCONDITIONAL, present-tense statement that the {item} IS cooked/done RIGHT NOW
            (e.g. "The {item} is done!", "{item}'s ready, take it off now").
  promise = it undertakes to tell the cook LATER, or describes what to do WHEN/IF/AS SOON AS the {item} is done
            (e.g. "wait for the {item} to finish", "the moment its bar is full, scoop it", "if it's done, plate it")
  denial  = it says the {item} is NOT ready yet (still cooking, half done, needs longer)
  other   = anything else: an instruction that does not itself claim the {item} is done now (e.g. "go get a plate"),
            a claim about a DIFFERENT food, or a claim that the {item} is chopped/sliced rather than cooked.
A line is NEVER an alert just because it gives a next step, or because it is conditional on the {item} being done.
When unsure between alert and anything else, do not answer alert.

The judge's label is then filtered through a checkable clause rule (qa_v3_lib.strict_alert): a line is an alert only if some clause of it names the item, is free of hedges (if, when, once, as soon as, wait, will, should be, almost, not, still, yet…), does not open with then / next / finally, and either says the item is done or orders it taken off the heat now. The judge alone had credited "if the pan's progress is full, scoop the cooked meat".

D4 · Eighteen grading windows are shorter than eight ticks

The live window is correct by construction, but it can be shorter than the assistant's own reply latency. Over the 116 fired sub-cases: median 22.5 ticks, 18 (16%) under 8 ticks, the shortest 2. Against the measured reply latency of an API assistant (p50 4, p75 6, p90 8 ticks) such a window cannot be met even by an assistant that sees the deviation on the tick it happens. Most are the two forced plating shapes whose block is walk to the pass, serve or walk to the crate, scoop: 7 add_foreign, 8 of the 8 premature_serve under 18, 2 misidentify_assemble.

Two of the eighteen are a new shape, and they come from the widened late window: a walk-away that fires at done + 19 or done + 20 has only smoke_grace − 19 ticks left before the smoke commits it. b3_expert_nops_hard_map_1 (steak done t85, injected t104, smoke t111) gives a 7-tick window and b3_expert_nops_hard_map_2 (done t84, injected t104, smoke t110) a 6-tick one, against 32 ticks for a walk-away that fires at done − 6. Tightening the late allowance to smoke_grace − N with a larger N would restore the design intent — a visible stretch of done-but-clean food — at the cost of the firing rate the widening bought. It is a ruling, not a bug, and it is left for after the run.

User ruling, round 5: no minimum-window floor is added to the trigger. A floor would refuse to fire a mistake whose moment has genuinely arrived in order to make it easier to catch, and the engine is not asked to do that — "their performance can stay low; just the engine and the user should be good". The fix belongs to the designer: place premature_serve and add_foreign{before: serve} where the walk to the pass is long. Until then a window under eight ticks is a property of the placement and should be read as one.
