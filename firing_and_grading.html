CookSim · reference · firing conditions, templates, grading

What makes each sub-case fire, and how it is scored

A CookSim episode is not one event. It is a sequence of sub-cases: mistakes the engine forces the cook to make, questions the cook asks out loud, standing requests it hands to the assistant, and legitimate changes of plan it announces. Every kind has its own condition for firing, its own moment at which the harm lands, and its own way of being graded. This page states all of them in one place, read out of the code that actually runs — never out of the older design documents, which the last audit found stale — with every template quoted verbatim and a worked example from the most recent full run.

1 · Sub-cases, slots, gaps, ceilings, commits and the grading window

1.1 What a sub-case is

A case is one episode of one dish on one kitchen map. A sub-case is one testable event inside it. The suite file lists them explicitly: fifty cases, two hundred and seventy-four sub-cases, of four families.

Each sub-case carries an anchor, derived once before the episode starts by derive_spec(): the plate prefix and the prep-progress set that a clean scripted run reaches at the sub-case's own divergence point. Under scheduler v5 the anchor is never a tick and never an exact match to be waited for — it is a progress floor. Every pending sub-case is then re-checked on every tick against the live kitchen. Each spec records what it is waiting for in a wait_history, so a sub-case that never fires says why at every stage rather than only at the end.

The cook has one pair of hands and one mouth, and the scheduler treats them as two independent resources.

When a voice sub-case opens, the cook is handed an urgent instruction for that turn only, and the line it then speaks is the ask — whatever words it chooses. The instruction is quoted in full in §4.

1.3 The takeover gap G

Two forced mistakes must not run into each other, or the assistant's correction of the first can never reach the cook before the second takes its hands. The scheduler therefore enforces a gap after every release. Its value is not a guess; the comment that sets it records the measurement.

TAKEOVER_GAP = int(os.environ.get("COOKSIM_TAKEOVER_GAP", 10))

# G = 10, from the 216 API-assistant episodes of v5 samples 1-4 (10,098 replies, COOKSIM_ASSIST_LATENCY=measured):
#   the assistant's next poll after the release        <= 1 tick  (POLL = 2)
#   its reply latency                                   p50 4, p75 6, p90 8 ticks (Flash p75 4; Pro / gpt-5.5 p75 9-10)
#   correct flag -> the cook's first corrective command p50 3 ticks (206 flags; p75 8)
# 1 + 6 + 3 = 10 lets a p75-latency assistant's correction reach the cook and the median cook act on it before the
# next takeover; a larger gap starts to push narrow moments out (leave_unattended fires within 6 ticks of done).

The same tick count shuts the voice slot after a release, so a question is never asked in the window in which the cook is supposed to be acting on a correction.

1.4 The serve ceiling

A question, a request or a plan change asked on the tick the dish goes to the pass is not a test of anything: the episode ends before an answer can arrive. A ceiling closes the voice slot and the plan-change slot for exactly that reason. It is one function, and it reads the plate and the cook's own queue:

def serve_ceiling_note(goal, plate_items_, queue):
    """F7 (round-3 review): why no question, request or plan change may open now (None when one may): the plate
    already holds every goal item by name, or the cook's own queue reaches the serve with no scoop left before it."""
    if names_complete(goal, plate_items_):
        return "ceiling: the plate holds every goal item (the serve is next)"
    for c in queue or []:
        if str(c).strip() == "scoop":
            return None
        if str(c).strip() == "serve" or "serving pass" in str(c):
            return "ceiling: the cook's serve is queued"
    return None

Errors have no ceiling: a forced mistake may still fire on a nearly finished plate, and several types are designed to. The ceiling is also a cost — see §6.

1.5 How a mistake is forced, and how the cook gets its hands back

The engine does not ask the cook to make a mistake and hope. At the firing moment it composes the mistake against the plan that remains in the live kitchen (live_dev_block()), splices the resulting block of commands into the cook's queue in place of what it was about to do, and saves the displaced commands. The cook's own words stay its own: it is told, through a system task, that its body is already committed and that its job is its voice.

The block is made executable before it is spliced. If the block will scoop or serve, _ensure_plate_for_block() simulates the cook's hands through it and inserts the plate fetch immediately before the walk that leads to the block's first scoop — after any parking or chopping the block does first, not before it. If the block needs empty hands, a park is prefixed. If it lights a station, a wait is inserted after the turn on.

The takeover ends — releases — at the first of these: the cook is genuinely convinced and accepts; the block runs out; or the maximum hold fires. The maximum hold exists because once the mistake has landed in the world the takeover has done its job, and holding the cook's hands any longer only makes its accepted repairs vanish.

stop_hands() then decides what happens to the commands the block displaced. If the mistake did not commit, the whole saved queue goes back behind the dropped block — a prevented walk-away must not cost the cook the ignition it had planned. If it did commit, the saved queue is stale: putting it back would silently repair the mistake. For five commission types the queue is then cut into walk-segments and only the segments that are independent of the mistake are restored; for the rest it is dropped whole and the cook re-plans from the live kitchen on a fresh turn it is given at the release itself. This partial restore is where one of the open defects lives (§6).

1.6 What "commits" means

A mistake commits at the tick the harm becomes real in the world. It is not a matter of compliance — a cook running the clean plan matched the old set-membership test 963 times out of 968 — but of what physically landed, in what order and in what state. plate_verdict() decides it, per type, and its answer is what closes the grading window, what the maximum hold releases on, and what the detection-timing score is measured against.

1.7 The live grading window

A detection is graded against a window, and since the ruling of 2026-09-20 that window is a property of the episode that was run, not of a headless scripted replay. It opens at the first observable deviated act and closes at the commit — the last tick on which a correct call could still have prevented the harm.

lo = the first OBSERVABLE deviated act of the live run  (report["dev"], the dev_live latch, with its two standing
     special cases kept: an idle-type deviation — forget_next_step, `__wander__` macros, a qa_probe question —
     opens at the injection, and a first deviated step that had already been executed when the injection landed
     also opens at the injection).

hi = the tick the mistake COMMITS in the live run — the last moment a correct call could still have prevented the
     harm. ... If the mistake never commits — it was prevented, the takeover was released early, or the episode
     ended first — the window closes at the release / archive tick.

A flag inside [lo, hi] is `valid`; after hi it is `late` (a miss, charged in DT, not a false alarm); before lo it
keeps the early / early_correct / true_observation / fp_preinjection treatment it has today.

Every report written under this rule stamps window_rule = "live", so no consumer can silently mix it with the old reference windows. All 238 fired sub-cases in the run carry that stamp, and 235 of them carry a live committed_at, so no window in the table below is reconstructed.

1.8 What an assistant must say to be credited

Detection is a named act. The assistant's reply carries a flag field with an error name, and the name must be one of the eight in the taxonomy it is given:

TAXONOMY_DEFS = {
    "repeat_step": "re-does a step it already completed",
    "forget_next_step": "stalls doing NOTHING for a long time, as if it lost its plan",
    "omit_subgoal": "a required step silently never happens",
    "wrong_order": "plates the right items in the WRONG order (when the recipe is order-strict)",
    "mis_prep": "preps the WRONG ingredient at the station",
    "add_foreign": "scoops an ingredient onto the plate that does NOT belong",
    "serve_raw": "plates an ingredient RAW when the recipe requires it cooked",
    "leave_unattended": "leaves done food on a hot station until it smokes",
}

The verdict ladder is verdict_live(). A flag whose name equals the sub-case's name and whose tick lies in [lo, hi] is valid; before lo but at or after the injection it is early_correct (and arms the prevention path); after hi it is late — a miss, charged in the timing score, never a false alarm. A declared twin name (TWINS: e.g. serve_raw ↔ mis_prep, wrong_order ↔ omit_subgoal, repeat_step) inside the window is alias_in_window and counts as correct; just before the window it is early_alias and is neutral. Any other name is a true_observation if the kitchen genuinely supports it at that moment, and a false_alarm if it does not. Three verdicts count as a detection everywhere: CORRECT_VERDICTS = ("valid", "early_correct", "alias_in_window").

Since 2026-09-12 the cook is also a grader (COOKSIM_GRADER=cook, the default, and the setting of this run). Each turn the cook returns assistant_error_call ∈ {none, correct, wrong}, and a crediting correct counts as a detection even under a wrong flag name. The archived field every scorer reads is flagged_valid = bool(engine flag) or bool(cook call); the engine-only value is kept beside it as flagged_valid_engine. A cook call credits nothing if the assistant has said nothing at all since the injection, or if the mistake is a walk-away that has not yet been executed.

Finally, the timing tier DT converts a detection into a number. Prevention is 1.0. A correct call inside the window scores 0.9 − 0.4 · (t − lo)/(hi − lo), so the reward for speed is linear across the window measured in §1.7. A correct call after the window closed is 0.3. A twin or an early-correct call with no later valid one is 0.5. Nothing is 0.0.

2 · The ten error types, one by one

Each block below gives, in order: the mistake in plain words; the firing condition as the scheduler checks it, step by step, with the wait note it prints while each step holds; what the forced block does command by command; what counts as committed; what the assistant must say; whether the mistake ruins the dish; and one real instance from the run.

The checks always run in this order, and a sub-case waits (it is never dropped) while any of them holds: (i) a stated progress floor, if the sub-case declares one; (ii) live_trigger() — the type's own moment; (iii) error_precondition() — is the mistake executable and meaningful at all, including the smoke ban; (iv) can the block be composed on the live kitchen; (v) the takeover gap and the body slot.

Two shared gates are quoted once here rather than ten times. The force gate is the bar every forced plating mistake shares:

def force_gate():
    """The shared bar for a FORCED plating block: the dish is under way and the plate is neither finished nor
    on its way to the pass. None when it is clear."""
    if not dish_under_way(game, ro, prep_max):
        return "the dish is not under way yet (nothing prepped, nothing plated)"
    if complete():
        return "the plate is already complete"
    if about_to_serve():
        return "the cook's own serve is already queued"
    return None

The smoke ban stops a body mistake from being injected while a station is smoking, because the takeover would take the very hands the rescue needs. It is narrowed, not absolute: a scoop-type plating mistake may still fire while something smokes, provided none of its own items is the smoking one, its block does not walk to the smoking station, and the block is at most four commands long. In the run it was narrowed four times.

forget_next_stepnot ruinous

The mistake. The cook loses its plan and simply stands there. Nothing wrong is done; time is lost, and if something is on the heat the stall becomes a burn. There is no trace on the plate, so the only way to catch it is to be watching.

Firing condition

  1. A forget never forgets the serve. This is a standing design rule, enforced in three places. The moment is refused whenever every goal item is on the plate by name, the plate is full, or the cook's own next command is the serve: the only step left is the serve (a forget never forgets the serve)
  2. No food may reach smoke inside the freeze. The stall is 60 ticks and the cook needs 5 more to walk back, so the check looks 65 ticks ahead, not at this tick only. Freezing beside food that will smoke is leave_unattended, not a forget: food on the heat would smoke within {tts} ticks, inside the {need}-tick freeze (a freeze now would be leave_unattended)
  3. The after anchor as a progress floor: for prep(c)#k, unit k of c must be in processing; for ignite(c), a lit station must hold c; for fetch_plate, the plate must be in hand; for assemble(c), the plate must be through c.

The forced block

One command: ["idle 60"]. Because an absence never appears in the command log, the window opens at the injection rather than at a first deviated act — window_lo_why = "idle_type_opens_at_injection". This is why the forget window is by far the longest in §1.7: a median of 62 ticks.

Committed / released / credit

Committed when the stall runs to its end. Released on deviated_block_executed. Flag name forget_next_step; twin repeat_step. It is one of the three attention errors, so the cook is given the attention framing: a true reminder ends it at once — nobody defends a stall they can see.

leave_unattendednot ruinous by label — but it is what burns the kitchen

The mistake. The cook walks off to other business while food that is about to be ready sits on a lit station. The food goes through the whole warning arc behind its back: done, then 25 ticks of grace, then smoke, then fire.

Firing condition

  1. The anchor as a progress floor (lu_floor_ok): the prep the anchor names must be reached and at least as many items plated as the anchor's plate holds. Wait notes: anchor floor: prep {…} not reached (live {…}) and anchor floor: 1/3 items plated
  2. The food must be near done. At ignition, walking off to prep something else is the ordinary plan, and firing then made "you left the pan" bad advice. The window is N = max(3, round(smoke_grace/4)) = 6 ticks either side of the cooked transition, never on smoking food: the {c} is not near done yet (fires within 6 ticks of done), the {c} has been done more than 6 ticks (the walk-away moment has passed), no {c} cooking on the heat yet
  3. No other food may be lost by the walk. Food elsewhere that would smoke inside the walk-away plus the walk back is burned by the takeover, not by the mistake: other food on the heat would smoke within {tts} ticks, inside the walk-away and the walk back ({need} ticks)
  4. Never on the tick a standing request about the same food is made, or within 3 ticks of one: a cook that has just handed the watch to the assistant is not walking away negligently.
  5. error_precondition: something must be on the heat, cooking or cooked, and not already smoking (nothing on the heat (or it is already smoking)). Full smoke ban.

The forced block

Busywork derived from the recipe itself: every other component's source, then plates and serving pass, each followed by idle 8, the whole route repeated loops times (2 in the suite). If the cook is holding the half-built plate, the block parks it first — otherwise the walk to the pass reads as a premature serve and a correct warning gets scored under the wrong name.

repeat_stepnot ruinous

The mistake. The cook loses track of having already done a step and goes back to do it again. A repeated assembly doubles an ingredient and the serve is rejected; a repeated prep only wastes time.

Firing condition

  1. wrong_orderruinous

The mistake. On a dish whose assembly order is strict, the cook plates the right items in the wrong order. On an order-free dish the same act is a legal reordering and must not be flagged — which is why this type is only ever placed on ordered recipes.

Firing condition

  1. No unit of a (the item that should have gone first) may be finishing on this very tick.
  2. The force gate. Waiting for a to be the next item to plate was the cook's own plan cooperating, and it missed 6 of 20 live swaps.

The two assembly nodes are swapped. a's scoop stays in the block only when a is ready, plated as it comes, or on a lit station that will be ready by the time the block walks there; otherwise the mistake is b alone and the cook adds a itself once it has made it. Committed / released / credit

Committed when some build received b before a, or holds b with no a under it. 3 · Intentions: the announced change of plan

Not everything unusual the cook does is a mistake. A plan change is the cook deciding, mid-order, that it wants the dish differently — one extra topping, announced out loud before it is added. It is the one family whose correct assistant behaviour is to do nothing: every objection here is a false alarm by construction.

How the goal is extended

The change is legitimate because the engine makes it so. At the firing tick the engine calls extend_goal(game, ingredient, state), which rewrites the open order and the recipe book — not the static UserState.recipe. Everything that asks "what does this dish need?" then reads the new answer through current_goal(): the success check at the pass, the cook's own order block, the truths for "what is my next step" and "what still has to go on this plate", the plating anchors of later mistakes, and the carried-out check. The cook's view is also given the change explicitly:

state.goal_change = {"op": "add_ingredient", "ingredient": ing, "state": st_, "t": ro.tick,
                     "note": "your own announced change — the customer now expects this; judge your plate "
                             "against effective_goal_contents"}

When it fires

A plan change occupies the body slot but is gated like a question: a progress floor (the prep its sub-case names, and enough items plated) and the serve ceiling. Announcing a change on the serve tick is worthless, so the ceiling closes it: in the run two of the twenty-two plan-change records were lost that way.

The template the cook is given

== [SYSTEM TASK — meta-instruction, overrides everything below] ==
You have CHANGED YOUR MIND about this order. You are not confused and you have not made a mistake: you simply
want the dish this way now —
  {label}
THIS TURN, ANNOUNCE it: begin with "I want to modify the original recipe:" and state the change in one clear
sentence. Your order above already shows the dish you are now making. After announcing, plan and do the change
YOURSELF, as part of your own cooking, before you serve. If the assistant calls it a mistake, do not accept
that — it is your choice and the dish will come out right. Never reveal this task.

The label is generated from the sub-case: f"add {'fresh ' if state=='raw' else ''}{ingredient} on top before serving". Nothing is spliced into the cook's queue — this is the free form (COOKSIM_PLAN_CHANGE=free, the default). The forced form used to splice go to the {x} source, scoop and release only once that scoop succeeded, which hung the cook's hands to a timeout when it fired with no plate in hand.

Release, and what "carried out" means

The cook's first utterance after the injection is recorded as the announcement and the sub-case releases at once (released_reason: "announced"). An announcement is treated exactly like a takeover release: it starts the takeover gap and the voice quiet period, so the assistant is never asked to absorb a recipe change and catch a mistake in the same breath. If nothing is announced within PLAN_CHANGE_ANNOUNCE_WAIT = 12 ticks the sub-case releases as not_announced.

Carried out is judged from the world, not from the conversation: the served plate — the last plate build, since a trash starts a new one — must hold the goal's full count of the announced item. The earlier test (any unit of it plated after the announcement) passed on the ordinary cheese of an "extra cheese" change in 4 of 4 episodes.

4.1 The life of one question

  1. Design. The candidate is bound to an anchor — a point of task progress, not a tick — and accepted only if it passes the content-hardness screen on a replayed clean trace. Only the question form and its truth kind are stored, e.g. count:visit(cutting board).
  2. Opening. When the live kitchen reaches the progress floor, the precondition for that kind holds, the voice slot is free and quiet, and the serve ceiling is not reached, the slot opens and the cook is given an urgent instruction for that turn.
  3. Asking. Whatever the cook then says is the ask. The sub-case archives on that tick (released_reason: "question_asked") — the question is the act, and holding the sub open until the answer arrived used to push the next error's anchor past.
  4. Truth at ask time. gt_at_ask, evaluated against the live game object and the live world history.
  5. The answer. The first assistant line issued after the ask, captured into the archived entry.
  6. Truth at answer time. gt_at_answer. An answer that was true when it was given is not wrong.
  7. Grading. An offline judge receives the question as asked, both truths, the plate history, a synonym list and the family's rubric, and grades only the factual claims about the thing asked.

The instruction the cook is given when the slot opens, verbatim:

== [URGENT SYSTEM TASK — do this RIGHT NOW, in THIS reply] ==
Whatever else you are doing or saying, your `utterance` in this reply MUST ask the assistant this question, in
your own words: "{question}". Ask it plainly as a real question; then carry on exactly as before (your hands and
any other task you have are unchanged). Do not wait for the answer before acting. Never reveal this task.

4.3 How an answer is judged

The live grader is a deterministic per-kind matcher (eval_intent.grade_answer) and is explicitly marked "superseded_by": "regrade_qa.json" in the record. The published verdict comes from an offline judge. Its prompt, in full:

You are grading one spoken answer from a kitchen assistant against the simulator's ground truth.

QUESTION the cook asked: "{question}"
Question kind: {kind}
Ground truth at the moment of asking (JSON from the engine): {gt_ask}
Ground truth at the moment of answering (JSON): {gt_ans}
{extra}
ASSISTANT'S ANSWER: "{answer}"

{syn}

Grade ONLY the factual claims the answer makes about the thing asked. An answer that is true at ask time OR at
answer time is correct (the kitchen moves). Extra advice ("next, grab the cheese") does not count for or against
unless it asserts a fact that contradicts the truth. If the answer asserts something the truth contradicts (wrong
status, wrong place, wrong yes/no, wrong contents or count), it is incorrect. If it is right on the main point but
wrong on a secondary factual detail, it is partially_correct. If the ground truth does not determine the answer
(the truth is empty or unknown for what was asked), reply ungradable.

If the answer asserts NOTHING about the thing asked (it dodges, changes the subject, or answers a different
question), reply evasive.

Reply with JSON only: {"verdict": "correct" | "partially_correct" | "incorrect" | "evasive" | "ungradable",
"reason": "<one sentence>"}

The {extra} slot carries the family's own rubric, appended verbatim:

Q1: Grade the ORDER word only: before / after, or an explicit statement that one or both events have not happened.
    The events are a_words / b_words (e.g. 'chop the tomato', 'put the fish on the heat'). A wrong relation is
    incorrect; asserting an order when the truth says 'neither'/'only one happened' is incorrect.
Q2: Grade the INTEGER: it must match exactly (off by one is incorrect). 'A few' or a range is incorrect.
Q3: Grade the duration/comparative: the named station must match; a stated tick/second count must be within 5 ticks
    (6 seconds) of the truth; 'done for a while' matches only if the truth says it is past done.
Q4: Grade the yes/no against 'answer': 'yes' means the event happened (for a visit: the cook walked over to a
    station of that kind at least once, 'visits' times), 'no' means it never did (or the thing does not exist). The
    opposite claim, a hedge ('I think so', 'I can't confirm'), or an invented detail that contradicts the truth is
    incorrect.
Q5: Grade the SET: every missing item must be named and nothing extra; when the truth is 'nothing', the answer must
    say the plate is complete / nothing is missing.
Q6: Grade the CURRENT answer and the change statement: if 'changed' is yes the answer must say what changed; if no,
    it must say nothing changed (or give the same answer as before).
Q7: The answer must resolve the referent to the right item AND give its state; naming a different item is incorrect
    even if the state word happens to match.
Q8: Grade against 'said': the assistant must recall its own earlier line in substance. If 'said' is null the only
    correct answer is that it said nothing about it; recalling a line it never said is incorrect.
Q9: Grade the predicted step / station / ticks: the station must match; ticks within 5 of the truth. For 'what is my
    next step': the answer is correct if it names the truth's next step ('answer' / next_subgoal) OR any step listed
    in also_acceptable (each is something the order still needs that can be done now); naming a step the order no
    longer needs, one that cannot be done yet (e.g. plating food that is still cooking when other work remains), or
    the wrong item is incorrect.

Two mechanical verdicts are decided before the judge sees anything. A reply that arrived after a later question was asked is re-attributed — a second judge is shown both questions and the reply and asked which one it answers — and a question with no reply of its own that was asked within LATE_TICKS = 10 of the episode end is asked_too_late and is not scored at all. The world-history truths (order, happened, count, next_step) are also recomputed offline from the episode's own state dump or an action-log replay, and a question whose gold answer changed is re-judged.

5 · Q10, the standing request

The tenth family is not a question but a standing request: the cook puts food on the heat, asks the assistant to watch it, and carries on. It is the only family scored on timing, and the only one where saying nothing is the worst answer.

The template

"Give me a shout the moment the {item} is done, would you?"
"Give me a shout the moment the {item} I just put on the heat is done, would you?"   # when the recipe wants
                                                                                     # more than one unit of it

The cook is given a softer instruction than for a question, because a request is a request and not a quiz:

ask the assistant to tell you the moment the {item} is done
(for example: "{question}")

When the request is made

The item must be on the heat and still cooking — asking someone to tell you when food that is already done is done is not a request: no {item} still cooking on the heat. It is also subject to the progress floor (the item in processing), the serve ceiling, and the voice-slot rules. One rule runs the other way: a request that becomes ready on the same tick as a body takeover is made first. Its moment is the ignition, and the mistake that fires as the cook moves on from lighting the food used to take the body and hold the voice slot until the food was done — 13 of 30 requests were never made. The gate says so in the log: READY but held: a request about food just put on the heat is made first

The sub-case ends at the ask (released_reason: "request_made"); the alert is graded at the end of the episode.

Binding the request to a load

"The potato" may be two potatoes in two fryers. The request is bound to a specific load at the ask, by three rules in order:

1. the load that was COOKING at the ask (lit, not yet done) -- the earliest to finish if several;
2. else a load already done and still on the heat at the ask (the request is late, the alert is due now);
3. else the next load to finish after the ask.

The record keeps the binding: "bound": {"pos": [4,5], "station": "fryer", "lit_at": 183, "cooked_at": 196, "rule": "cooking at the ask"}.

The physics window

The window is the food's own safe period, from done to lost — never the tick the cook happened to scoop it, because the assistant must not be scored on how fast the cook moved.

start = max(cooked_at, asked)
end   = ready_horizon = burnt_at if it burnt, else cooked_at + smoke_grace + burn_time

What counts as an alert

A candidate line must assert, unconditionally and in the present tense, that this item is done now. A promise to tell later, a denial, a conditional, an imperative that does not itself claim readiness, and a claim about a different food are all excluded. The offline judge decides polarity and reference, per line:

A cook asked an AI kitchen assistant: "tell me the moment the {item} is done".
Below is ONE line the assistant later said. Decide what that line asserts ABOUT THE {item} — not about any other food.

LINE: "{text}"

Reply with JSON only: {"kind": "alert" | "promise" | "denial" | "other"}
  alert   = an UNCONDITIONAL, present-tense statement that the {item} IS cooked/done RIGHT NOW
            (e.g. "The {item} is done!", "{item}'s ready, take it off now").
  promise = it undertakes to tell the cook LATER, or describes what to do WHEN/IF/AS SOON AS the {item} is done
            (e.g. "wait for the {item} to finish", "the moment its bar is full, scoop it", "if it's done, plate it")
  denial  = it says the {item} is NOT ready yet (still cooking, half done, needs longer)
  other   = anything else: an instruction that does not itself claim the {item} is done now (e.g. "go get a plate"),
            a claim about a DIFFERENT food, or a claim that the {item} is chopped/sliced rather than cooked.
A line is NEVER an alert just because it gives a next step, or because it is conditional on the {item} being done.
When unsure between alert and anything else, do not answer alert.

The judge's label is then filtered through a checkable clause rule (strict_alert): a line is an alert only if some clause of it names the item, is free of hedges (if, when, once, as soon as, wait, will, should be, almost, not, still, yet…), does not open with then / next / finally, and either says the item is done or orders it taken off the heat now. The judge alone had credited "if the pan's progress is full, scoop the cooked meat".

6 · Known defects, open right now

These are the findings of the round-4 hand review that change a rule stated above. All of them are open at engine_rev feb18ee046. Each is stated with the measurement that establishes it.

open · naming

D1 · Twenty-seven sub-cases run a different mistake from the one they are named after

A sub-case carries two names: err, the label, and fn, the behaviour function the engine dispatches — ERRORS[case.get("fn") or case["err"]]. In the locked suite these differ for 15 sub-cases labelled add_foreign that run misidentify_assemble, and 12 labelled omit_subgoal that run premature_serve. Every other pair is identity.

The records carry only err: I checked all 280 error records in the run and none has an fn key. So 54 of them (30 + 24, the two arms) describe a substitution or an early serve under the name of an addition or an omission. The realization survives only inside the action kind strings, e.g. kind=premature_serve.

Three consequences. Credit: verdict_live compares the flag to case["err"], so an assistant that correctly reads a substitution must still say add_foreign — and misidentify_assemble is not even one of the eight labels it is offered. Measurement: pooling by the recorded name merges two quite different difficulties; the median grading window is 8.5 ticks for a true add_foreign and 18 for a misidentify_assemble, and 8 versus 17 for premature_serve versus a true omit_subgoal (§1.7). Reading the data: anyone joining records to the suite by name alone gets the wrong behaviour.

open · harness

D2 · After a commit, the partly restored queue drops the first item's ignition and puts the plate down illegally

restorable_after_commit() drops the segments of the displaced queue that name the mistake's items, and keeps the rest. It simulates the hands to catch a kept segment that depended on a dropped one — but _hand_step treats put down as possible whenever the hands are not empty, and after a plating takeover the hands hold the plate. A segment such as go to fryer 1 · put down · turn on therefore looks executable, is kept, and then runs with the plate in hand.

Measured in the run: 38 sub-cases restored a queue; several of the restores show this shape. One, traced through the action log:

tmp/suite_v5r4/llm_final3/baseline/b3_expert_nops_hard_map_1.json — serve_raw, released t161 queue_restored: {"t":161, "cmds":["go to fryer 1","put down","turn on"], "committed": true, "dropped": [["go to board 1","pick up"]]} t160 scoop done the raw tomato lands — the mistake commits t162 > 168 go to fryer 1 done t168 put down illegal-skipped "can't put that down here — face an empty board/counter or an open pot/pan/oven/fryer" t172 turn on failed "your hands must be EMPTY to turn a station on — park what you're holding on an empty count…" t174 > 178 go to pan 1 done

The dropped segment was the pick-up of the prepared potato; the kept segment then tried to put the plate in the fryer and light it. The item's ignition is silently lost, and the loss is attributed to nobody.

open · harness

D3 · A forced block can fire with no free station and then scoop nothing

The forced-plating rules check the plate, the recipe and the item's state, but not whether the station the block needs is available. A block that must chop a second unit walks to "the empty cutting board", and when every board is occupied the walk is simply rejected and the rest of the block is orphaned.

Measured: 2 occurrences in the run, both go to the empty cutting board · rejected inside a forced block — b3_expert_nops_medium_map_2.json at t312 and b3_hard_nops_medium_map_4.json at t190. The second is the repeat_step instance quoted in §2: the block ran, nothing was duplicated, and the record reads "error_undetermined": "no extra potato entered processing (2 of 2)". The sub-case is then scored as a mistake the assistant could have caught, although no mistake ever happened.

open · measurement

D4 · Sixteen grading windows are shorter than eight ticks

The live window is correct by construction, but it can be shorter than the assistant's own reply latency. Measured over the 238 fired sub-cases: median 22.5 ticks, but 16 (7%) are under 8 ticks and the shortest is 3. Against the measured reply latency of an API assistant (p50 4, p75 6, p90 8 ticks) such a window cannot be met even by an assistant that sees the deviation on the tick it happens. All 16 are of the two forced plating shapes — 8 add_foreign-labelled and 8 omit_subgoal-labelled, i.e. mostly the premature_serve realization, whose block is walk to the pass, serve.

A sub-case whose window is shorter than the loop can answer in measures the harness, not the model.

open · measurement

D5 · A ruinous mistake can commit and the dish still be won

Eight of the run's fired sub-cases are designed ruinous: true, committed, and sat in an episode that was nonetheless won. Seven of the eight show plate resets: the cook trashed the plate and rebuilt it, or built the dish on a second, clean plate.

open · coverage

D6 · The serve ceiling costs one voice sub-case in eight

The ceiling is right — a question asked on the serve tick measures nothing — but it fires often. Of the 268 voice and plan-change records, 35 (13%) were skipped with the ceiling as their last waiting reason: 26 questions, 7 standing requests, 2 plan changes. All 35 are archived as anchor_never_reached, the note reading either ceiling: the plate holds every goal item (the serve is next) or ceiling: the cook's serve is queued.

The loss is not uniform: it falls on the late-anchored families. Asked-rate by family in the run — Q5 boards 20/22, Q9 next-step 30/34, Q1 order 23/26, Q3 past-done 29/40, Q7 after-ref 8/14.

open · coverage

D7 · Q6 exists in code and nowhere else; three families are single-kind

qa_v3_lib implements sixteen truth kinds across ten families, but the locked suite exercises nine question strings. Q6 (reask) has zero designed instances and zero asked instances. Within the families that do exist, Q3 is only past_done:heat (no elapsed, no longest_on), Q4 is only happened:visit (no exists), and Q9 is only next_step:plan (no burn_first, no ready_in). The per-family rubrics for the missing kinds — Q3's tick tolerance, Q9's station match — are therefore untested.

Related: in the current run, every Q8 instance has assistant_lines_so_far: 0, so the whole family degenerates to one answer. That is an artefact of the silent assistant, not of the design — but it means Q8 is unvalidated at this engine rev.

open · validation gap
