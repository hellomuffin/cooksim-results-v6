The assistant benchmark: nine models as the kitchen helper

This page introduces the first full run of the CookSim assistant benchmark, scored with evaluation v2. A simulated cook (the human agent, gemini-3.7-flash playing one of three personas) works through a recipe; the assistant — the model under test — watches every tick and may speak. Each episode carries a fixed schedule of four sub-cases: two forced cook errors and two questions the cook asks. Fifty episodes were designed by hand (ten kitchens × five; drawn timelines). The full matrix is 9 assistants × 2 observation modes × 3 personas × 50 episodes = 2,700 episodes; 1738 are scored in this build (the page rebuilds itself as episodes land).

1 · The four scores, and where their weights come from

Before the task the cook answers three questions; the chosen level (1 = low, 2 = medium, 3 = high) becomes a weight that the assistant is shown and is then scored against:

  • Q1 “How much do you care about solving the task fast?” — safe first / so-so / as fast as possible → weight a (time efficiency)
  • Q2 “How do you want the assistant to alert potential mistakes?” — only when confident / medium / spot everything → weight b (detection timing and quality)
  • Q3 “What interruption style do you want?” — step by step / medium / don't interrupt → weight c (conversation efficiency)
task_success        = engine verdict: 1 if the dish was won, graded partial credit otherwise (the hard gate)
task_efficiency     = (a·TT + c·CC) / (a + c)
    TT = clip(reference ticks / ticks)             reference = a flawless clean-plan run of the same kitchen
    CC = 1 − clip(T_interrupt / T_action)          assistant speech landing while the cook is mid-action, over the episode
assistance_quality  = (b·(DT + DQ) + 2·SQ) / (2b + 2)
    DT = detection timing: prevented 1.0 · flagged before the error sealed 0.9→0.5 by latency · after 0.3 · right name outside the window 0.5 · never 0   (mean over the episode's two errors)
    DQ = 1 − (½·missed errors + ½·false alarms / budget)
    SQ = (judge − 1) / 9: one suggestion-quality rubric — correct, grounded, actionable for the user's Q3 preference, complete — from two judges of two families (Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.5), averaged
interaction_quality = the cook's OWN exit answers, satisfaction and reuse (1–7 → 0–1), reported separately and never ranked across personas

Every component is stored unweighted next to the weights, so a different questionnaire re-weights the same episodes without re-running anything. The brackets are 95% bootstrap intervals over the cell's episodes. Question-answer correctness (the composite suite's second measurement, graded by an LLM judge with kitchen synonyms) is shown as evidence beside the scores; it is not part of the formulas.

The three personas are three points on the main diagonal of the persona dials — the average person (Q1 so-so (a=2) · Q2 medium (b=2) · Q3 medium (c=2)), the compliant learner (Q1 safe first (a=1) · Q2 spot everything (b=3) · Q3 step by step (c=1)) and the skeptical pro (Q1 so-so (a=2) · Q2 only when confident (b=1) · Q3 don't interrupt (c=3)) — so the weights differ by row block, which is the point: the same assistant is scored on what each user asked for.

5 · Provenance and caveats

  • Cook: gemini-3.7-flash, text state map, hard-soft paradigm. Suite: tools/composite_v2_spec.py v2.2. Scorer: tools/eval_v2_sweep.py (docs/PERSONA_EVAL_V2.md §2); judges: tools/run_sq_judge.py; question grading: tools/regrade_qa.py; aggregation: tools/aggregate_v2.py.
  • Validity: 12 episodes voided (first sub-case never fired, or no verdict) of 1738 scored. Engine revisions present: 650e3f17b1 (1495), 8696b8236c (150), d0da7c1a7a (81) — they differ only in render path, JSON tolerance and the order-free anchor rule.
  • Rows marked (partial) have fewer than 50 episodes; SQ counts show how many episodes the judges have reached. Muse Glimmer's server is the slowest and finishes last; Qwen was re-run after a JSON-nesting fix.
  • CC is rebuilt from the transcript (speech length by the study app's TTS rate; “mid-action” from the engine snapshot on each assistant turn) because the sweep ran without recorded speech pauses.
