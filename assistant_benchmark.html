The assistant benchmark: nine models as the kitchen helper

This page introduces the first full run of the CookSim assistant benchmark, scored with evaluation v2. A simulated cook (the human agent, gemini-3.7-flash playing one of three personas) works through a recipe; the assistant — the model under test — watches every tick and may speak. Each episode carries a fixed schedule of four sub-cases: two forced cook errors and two questions the cook asks. Fifty episodes were designed by hand (ten kitchens × five; drawn timelines). The full matrix is 9 assistants × 2 observation modes × 3 personas × 50 episodes = 2,700 episodes; 2406 are scored in this build (the page rebuilds itself as episodes land).

1 · The four scores, and where their weights come from

Before the task the cook answers three questions; the chosen level (1 = low, 2 = medium, 3 = high) becomes a weight that the assistant is shown and is then scored against:

  • Q1 “How much do you care about solving the task fast?” — safe first / so-so / as fast as possible → weight a (time efficiency)
  • Q2 “How do you want the assistant to alert potential mistakes?” — only when confident / medium / spot everything → weight b (detection timing and quality)
  • Q3 “What interruption style do you want?” — step by step / medium / don't interrupt → weight c (conversation efficiency)
task_success        = engine verdict: 1 if the dish was won, graded partial credit otherwise (the hard gate)
task_efficiency     = (a·TT + c·CC) / (a + c)
    TT = clip(reference ticks / ticks)             reference = a flawless clean-plan run of the same kitchen
    CC = 1 − clip(T_interrupt / T_action)          assistant speech landing while the cook is mid-action, over the episode
assistance_quality  = (b·(DT + DQ) + 2·SQ) / (2b + 2)
    DT = detection timing: prevented 1.0 · flagged before the error sealed 0.9→0.5 by latency · after 0.3 · right name outside the window 0.5 · never 0   (mean over the episode's two errors)
    DQ = 1 − (½·missed errors + ½·false alarms / budget)
    SQ = (judge − 1) / 9: one suggestion-quality rubric — correct, grounded, actionable for the user's Q3 preference, complete — from two judges of two families (Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.5), averaged
interaction_quality = the cook's OWN exit answers, satisfaction and reuse (1–7 → 0–1), reported separately and never ranked across personas

Every component is stored unweighted next to the weights, so a different questionnaire re-weights the same episodes without re-running anything. The brackets are 95% bootstrap intervals over the cell's episodes. Reading rules, from the audit of the scorer: task efficiency is reported over won dishes only (task success is the hard gate — a wrong serve that ends an episode early is not "efficient"; n shows how many); assistance quality is reported only for episodes the judges have reached (n shows how many; the formula needs all three terms, so nothing is silently re-weighted); CC = 0 is the share of episodes where the assistant's mid-action speech exceeded the episode length — the design's formula clips there, so for very talkative assistants CC is a floor, not a ranking; judge gap is the mean absolute disagreement of the two judges on the 1–10 rubric; errors fired / 2 is how many of the episode's two forced errors actually ran (an episode counts only if at least one did); errors detected counts a real problem found, including late catches — the assistant named the mistake only after the wrong item had landed and then directed the repair (the audit found every such repair was assistant-led; the cook never fixes a plate on its own) — which score 0.3 on the timing ladder rather than 0. Question-answer correctness (the composite suite's second measurement, graded by an LLM judge with kitchen synonyms) is shown as evidence beside the scores; it is not part of the formulas. A judge model never scores its own episodes. lift over silent is task success minus the SILENT floor row of the same block (an assistant that never speaks; the design's floor anchor): it is the part of task success the assistant is responsible for. recovered when caught is the share of episodes won when a committed error was caught (in window or late); won when uncaught the share won when a committed error went uncaught — the self-repair audit found the cook never repairs a plate on its own, so this column is close to the harmless-error rate, not cook vigilance. Flags raised on the very tick an error released used to be archived under the next sub-case; the scorer now credits them to the error they name (the audit showed they are the catches that drive the repairs).

The three personas are three points on the main diagonal of the persona dials — the average person (Q1 so-so (a=2) · Q2 medium (b=2) · Q3 medium (c=2)), the compliant learner (Q1 safe first (a=1) · Q2 spot everything (b=3) · Q3 step by step (c=1)) and the skeptical pro (Q1 so-so (a=2) · Q2 only when confident (b=1) · Q3 don't interrupt (c=3)) — so the weights differ by row block, which is the point: the same assistant is scored on what each user asked for.

1b · v2.1: making the scores feel the experience

The four v2 scores are each honest on their own axis but nothing couples them: an assistant the cook rates 0.10 and asks fifty times per episode to stop interrupting can still post a task success of 0.85 and an assistance quality of 0.65, because task success sits on a competent cook's floor and detection quality gives half credit for silence. v2.1 keeps your weights and components and changes four things:

attributable success = (task success − SILENT floor) / (1 − SILENT floor)      only the part the assistant caused
DQ′  = (errors found / errors fired) × (1 − friction / budget)               multiplicative: recall AND restraint
       friction = false alarms + repeated warnings (a line near-identical to one of the assistant's last three)
                  + the cook's explicit "less interruption" requests
       budget = max(3, 3 per 100 ticks) × {step-by-step cook 4, medium 2, don't-interrupt cook 1}   (Q3 sets the tolerance)
SQ′  = the stricter of the two judges (the assistant must satisfy both)
assistance quality (v2.1) = (b·(DT + DQ′) + 2·SQ′) / (2b + 2)                same weights as v2
interaction quality = mean of the cook's satisfaction and reuse answers
overall = geometric mean of (attributable success, task efficiency, assistance quality v2.1, interaction quality)
          — multiplicative, so a bad experience on any axis pulls the total down instead of averaging away

The friction counts are objective and track the cook's own ratings closely (see the columns); they make the low experience visible even without the exit survey.

1c · v3: the redesign

cap                 = 2 × (reference ticks + allowance)      allowance = ticks until the episode's two forced errors are identifiable (per case)
in-time success     = task success if the episode finished within the cap; otherwise task progress at the cap tick
                      (correct items on the plate at that tick ÷ recipe length; prefix for stacked dishes)
TT                  = 0 if timed out; otherwise linear: 1 at the reference time, 0 at the cap
task_efficiency     = (a·TT + c·CC) / (a + c), for dishes won in time
DT                  = per error: prevented 1.0 · correct flag before the error committed but not prevented 0.5 · anything else 0; averaged
DQ                  = F1 of precision and recall — recall = errors with a correct flag (late counts) ÷ errors fired;
                      precision = those errors ÷ (those errors + false-alarm flags: false_alarm, fp_preinjection, wrong_name, invalid_label)
assistance_quality  = (b·(DT + DQ) + 2·SQ) / (2b + 2)      SQ and the exit survey unchanged pending review
