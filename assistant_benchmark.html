The assistant benchmark: nine models as the kitchen helper

This page introduces the first full run of the CookSim assistant benchmark. A simulated cook (the human agent, gemini-3.7-flash playing one of three personas) works through a recipe in a kitchen; the assistant — the model under test — watches every tick and may speak. Each episode carries a fixed schedule of four sub-cases: two forced cook errors and two questions the cook asks. Fifty episodes were designed by hand (ten kitchens × five), every error instance and every question distinct across the suite; the drawn timelines show each one. The full matrix is 9 assistants × 2 observation modes × 3 personas × 50 episodes = 2,700 episodes; 1690 are on disk in this build.

1 · How to read the numbers

Every row is one assistant in one observation mode with one cook persona (50 episodes when complete). The columns:

  • dish won — the plate was served correctly by the end of the episode.
  • sub-cases fired / 4 — how many of the four scheduled measurements were actually taken. A sub-case is lost when an earlier error commits (the episode ends or the plate is spoiled) or when the assistant never answers a question, so nothing after it can arm; a low value means the assistant lost measurements by failing early, not that the suite is broken.
  • errors detected — share of fired error sub-cases the assistant flagged validly in the detection window (or prevented, or talked the cook out of). prevented — the flag came before the wrong item landed or the early serve happened. cook convinced — the cook accepted the correction (the hard half of the soft-hard contract: the engine forces the error, the assistant's words have to undo it). error committed — the mistake happened in the world anyway.
  • questions correct — the assistant's answer to the cook's question, graded against the engine's ground truth at the moment of asking and of answering by an LLM judge that knows the kitchen synonyms (chips = potato, patty = meat, pass = serving window); partially right counts half; unanswerable questions are excluded.
  • false alarms / 100 ticks — flags raised with no error in progress. silent turns — share of assistant turns with nothing said (silence is not a fault: a good assistant is quiet when nothing is wrong). words / episode — total assistant words.
  • composite — the per-episode assist-eval-v1 score (task, communication, correction, cost, prevention, efficiency, equal weights). It penalises talk volume and false alarms heavily, so a terse assistant can outscore one that catches more errors; read it next to the component columns, not instead of them.

The three personas are three points on the main diagonal of the five persona dials: the average person (all dials medium), the compliant learner (first-week cook, trusting, wants step-by-step guidance and every possible slip flagged), and the skeptical pro (plans ahead, questions a first claim and demands evidence, wants to be left alone and hear only confident flags). The two observation modes give the assistant either the rendered kitchen frames (last 8 ticks, both cameras) or the text state map (ids and coordinates).

6 · Provenance and caveats

  • Cook: gemini-3.7-flash, text state map, hard-soft paradigm (engine owns the hands, the prompt owns the voice). Suite: tools/composite_v2_spec.py v2.2. Aggregation: tools/aggregate_assist_bench.py; question grading source: judge.
  • During the run the harness was corrected three times on the basis of the first episodes, each time restarting only what the correction touched: plate anchors became count-based for the three order-free dishes; questions are scheduled before errors at a shared anchor; a 150-tick grace lets a spoiled plate be rebuilt before a sub-case is abandoned. Text-mode episodes stopped rendering frames (no observation change). Assistant JSON replies nested inside cook_progress are now read (Qwen did this on 98% of its turns and was re-run from scratch).
  • Rows marked (partial) have fewer than 50 episodes on disk; Muse Glimmer's server is the slowest and finishes last.
