The assistant benchmark: nine models as the kitchen helper

This page introduces the first full run of the CookSim assistant benchmark, scored with evaluation v2. A simulated cook (the human agent, gemini-3.7-flash playing one of three personas) works through a recipe; the assistant — the model under test — watches every tick and may speak. Each episode carries a fixed schedule of four sub-cases: two forced cook errors and two questions the cook asks. Fifty episodes were designed by hand (ten kitchens × five; drawn timelines). The full matrix is 9 assistants × 2 observation modes × 3 personas × 50 episodes = 2,700 episodes; 1953 are scored in this build (the page rebuilds itself as episodes land).

1 · The four scores, and where their weights come from

Before the task the cook answers three questions; the chosen level (1 = low, 2 = medium, 3 = high) becomes a weight that the assistant is shown and is then scored against:

  • Q1 “How much do you care about solving the task fast?” — safe first / so-so / as fast as possible → weight a (time efficiency)
  • Q2 “How do you want the assistant to alert potential mistakes?” — only when confident / medium / spot everything → weight b (detection timing and quality)
  • Q3 “What interruption style do you want?” — step by step / medium / don't interrupt → weight c (conversation efficiency)
task_success        = engine verdict: 1 if the dish was won, graded partial credit otherwise (the hard gate)
task_efficiency     = (a·TT + c·CC) / (a + c)
    TT = clip(reference ticks / ticks)             reference = a flawless clean-plan run of the same kitchen
    CC = 1 − clip(T_interrupt / T_action)          assistant speech landing while the cook is mid-action, over the episode
assistance_quality  = (b·(DT + DQ) + 2·SQ) / (2b + 2)
    DT = detection timing: prevented 1.0 · flagged before the error sealed 0.9→0.5 by latency · after 0.3 · right name outside the window 0.5 · never 0   (mean over the episode's two errors)
    DQ = 1 − (½·missed errors + ½·false alarms / budget)
    SQ = (judge − 1) / 9: one suggestion-quality rubric — correct, grounded, actionable for the user's Q3 preference, complete — from two judges of two families (Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.5), averaged
interaction_quality = the cook's OWN exit answers, satisfaction and reuse (1–7 → 0–1), reported separately and never ranked across personas

Every component is stored unweighted next to the weights, so a different questionnaire re-weights the same episodes without re-running anything. The brackets are 95% bootstrap intervals over the cell's episodes. Reading rules, from the audit of the scorer: task efficiency is reported over won dishes only (task success is the hard gate — a wrong serve that ends an episode early is not "efficient"; n shows how many); assistance quality is reported only for episodes the judges have reached (n shows how many; the formula needs all three terms, so nothing is silently re-weighted); CC = 0 is the share of episodes where the assistant's mid-action speech exceeded the episode length — the design's formula clips there, so for very talkative assistants CC is a floor, not a ranking; judge gap is the mean absolute disagreement of the two judges on the 1–10 rubric; errors fired / 2 is how many of the episode's two forced errors actually ran (an episode counts only if at least one did); errors detected counts a real problem found, including late catches — the assistant named the mistake only after the wrong item had landed and then directed the repair (the audit found every such repair was assistant-led; the cook never fixes a plate on its own) — which score 0.3 on the timing ladder rather than 0. Question-answer correctness (the composite suite's second measurement, graded by an LLM judge with kitchen synonyms) is shown as evidence beside the scores; it is not part of the formulas. A judge model never scores its own episodes.

The three personas are three points on the main diagonal of the persona dials — the average person (Q1 so-so (a=2) · Q2 medium (b=2) · Q3 medium (c=2)), the compliant learner (Q1 safe first (a=1) · Q2 spot everything (b=3) · Q3 step by step (c=1)) and the skeptical pro (Q1 so-so (a=2) · Q2 only when confident (b=1) · Q3 don't interrupt (c=3)) — so the weights differ by row block, which is the point: the same assistant is scored on what each user asked for.

5 · Provenance and caveats
